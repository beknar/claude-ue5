# random_dungeon_level and random_dungeoncombat_level (every dungeon level is a copy of these)

<!-- moved from CLAUDE.md (2026-10-23) to load only when these files are opened -->

- **random_dungeon_level:** C++ `ARDDungeonGenerator` / `ARDSection` (`Source/claude_ue5/RD`). Content is data: `770` fills the generator's `Pieces` map (each mesh normalised by `Adjust` to the dungeon frame: front +X, footprint centred, floor z 0) and the furnishing code places pieces by name, so a missing piece is skipped silently (check `770`'s piece list). Re-run `770` after C++ layout/furnishing changes (the start section is saved; play rebuilds only its doors, containers, monsters and map data from the same seed). Runtime sections are built over several frames (`FrameBudgetMs`). Every container must be openable (body + moving part); don't add closed barrels/crates/vases as static props. A moving part's mesh (lid, wardrobe door) has its origin at its hinge, in the body mesh's axes: on its hinge pivot it gets the piece's rotation and scale and no offset, and a piece's `Hinge` is where that origin goes on the body. `kit.json`'s `_hinges` and `interactive.json`'s `hinge` are written by Blender scripts and hold Blender's y (UE's is the negative). Both were wrong for a long time (every lid lay on the floor inside its body, every wardrobe door stood in the middle of the wardrobe) while the walk tests passed: they counted containers whose open value reached 1. `container_check.py` now measures the parts against the body (771, 781 at the end; 792's `containers` step). The Save As copies carry their own `Pieces` and their own saved start section: `770_rd_level.py pieces` rewrites the table of the open dungeon level and builds its start section again; `780_iv_level.py containers` rewrites the village's container pieces.
  - Looks that went wrong unnoticed in the dungeon, and what each was:
    - Torches, braziers and hearths gave light without fire: the pack's `P_TorchFire` was made with the Cascade-to-Niagara converter and calls that plugin's scripts. Without `CascadeToNiagaraConverter` enabled the system does not compile and draws nothing, with no error on screen (the log says `Unknown Function Call`). A Niagara effect that shows nothing: read the log first.
    - The flame is drawn round the effect's origin, not above it: scaled up for a brazier it hung below the bowl. Bigger fires go higher (`RDBrazierFlameUp`).
    - She held the sword by its blade: the mesh's origin is the tip of the blade (hilt at z 65 to 87), and the grip assumed "blade +Z from the origin". `790` now checks `hand_z` against the mesh (a round, thin handle there; the business end is the longer part). The shield's top goes toward the elbow, or it hangs point up when her arm hangs.
    - Under a container lid: the face that closes the cut had no UVs on the prop's atlas and showed the atlas's corner. It is on the `Interior` slot now (`mfv_lid_caps.py`).
    - The broken walls' holes go right through them, and behind every wall is an empty tile: through a hole she saw the void and the see-through backs of the rooms beyond, and could climb into the bigger holes and fall out of the dungeon. Each broken segment now has a backing (`Wall_Rough_Back`, an 8 cm stone plate 15.5 to 23.5 cm behind the wall line: at a section's border the next section's wall stands on the same line, the thinnest reaching 25 cm back, so the plate must stay inside that) and a seal (`Wall_Rough_Seal`, an invisible box to 30 cm in front of the line); the generator puts her back where she last stood if she drops 4 m below the floor (`FallResetDepth`). `wall_check.py` measures it with rays (the seals switched off: they block traces too) against the same rays without the plates.
    - Prison cells had bars only across the front: the cells beside the room's lane (the slots near it stay empty) were open on that side and neighbouring cells were one. And the cell door was hinged at +75 cm on a front whose half grating covers [-150, 0]: its 1.5 m leaf covered [-72, 75] twice and left a 75 cm gap at the corner. Bars now go on every side the room's wall does not close, and the hinge is 5 cm in from the corner (the leaf covers [-2, 148]). The hay piles (a 1.5 m mound that reads as rock) were placed without collision; their mesh collides as its triangles. Test cells with a swept ball, not a line: a line exactly through a blocker's middle at 90 degrees missed it.
    - One broken wall mesh along a whole room repeats its break every 3 m: a wall piece may have variants (`Name_2`, `Name_3`, ...), chosen by a hash of where the segment is, never by the plan's random stream (the layout must stay what it was).
    - The tests had passed all along: "the weapon's origin is within 130 cm of her hand" and "a light is there" were true. 792's `weapons` and `look` steps measure the handle under her hand, the shield's top, live flame particles, the wall pieces and the lid's cut face.
  - `791` builds the monsters again from the generator's own: any field another script sets on them (`797`'s `sound_set`, `step_length`) must be in its copy list, or a re-run silences them. random_dungeon_level_Cine (`773`-`775`): the generator follows `Demo_RD_Fem5` (`TrackedActor`, fixed seed) and opens doors in front of her (`TrackedAutoDoorDistance`); monsters don't block her (`bMonstersBlockPawns` off).
- **random_dungeoncombat_level:** a Save As copy of random_dungeon_level whose generator has `bCombat` and `bStylizedMap` on (`790`-`799`); the two levels share the C++ and `BP_RD_GameMode`, so anything new must stay behind those flags.
  - The player's side is `URDCombatComponent`, which the generator puts on the player pawn in play (the pawn is a Blueprint). Attacks are dynamic montages on her AnimBP's `DefaultSlot` (`PlaySlotAnimationAsDynamicMontage`); weapons are data on the generator (`Weapons`, from `790`'s weapons.json via `791`).
  - Her skeleton has no finger or hand-end bones (`righthand_end` is a clip track, not a bone: its socket location is the origin). Grips are computed from the reference pose (T-pose, palms down).
  - A monster's hit points and damage = the player's hit points / her average blow x the tier of its section (`StartMonster*`, `RuntimeMonster*`) x its kind's factor / the weighted mean of the factors. With `bCombat` the generator gives exactly `MonsterCoverage` of the rooms a monster (the old level flips a coin per room).
  - `ARDDungeonGenerator.RestartPlay(seed)` puts the dungeon back to its start with a fixed session seed (demo reel, tests). A step function of a generator-style test that never yields is not a generator: `yield from` on it fails.
  - A PowerShell here-string containing `(RM);` trips the permission layer's Remove-Item check: write such patches with the Write tool.
  - Blood (`URDBloodComponent`, made by the generator in play), the wounds and the sounds are data on the generator (`798`, `797`); a level without them is silent and bloodless. Sound and blood have random streams of their own (`SoundR`, the component's, a monster's `SR`): they must never draw from the streams the play uses, or a reel stops repeating.
  - Wounds: an overlay material instance per monster (`M_RDC_Wounds`, `SetOverlayMaterial`), its wounds stored in the mesh's reference pose (the material compares them with the pre-skinned position). The wound vertex is found by skinning a spread of LOD vertices on the CPU (`GetSkinnedVertexPosition`; the vertex buffers are readable in the editor; a packaged game keeps them unless `r.FreeSkeletalMeshBuffers` is set, which was not tried), with the nearest bone as the fallback.
  - This project has Substrate on: a pack material written for the old shading models may not compile (the engine then draws the default material without an error on screen: RealisticBlood's decals were pale squares; the log says `Failed to compile ... Default Material will be used`). A deferred decal needs a Substrate blend mode (`BLEND_TRANSLUCENT_GREY_TRANSMITTANCE`).
  - Building a material from Python logs `Failed to compile` for every half-built state (each `set_editor_property` on a node recompiles); only one after the final `recompile_material` is real (`798` logs a marker line before it). `get_statistics(...).num_pixel_shader_instructions > 0` does not prove the material compiled.
  - Sound: `Sounds` maps an occasion to a set (`sounds.json` -> `rdc_sound_prep.py` -> `797`). Placed sets are played at a location with an attenuation object made at run time per (inner radius, falloff); the listener's attenuation point is the pawn. The generator sets the audio listener itself every tick: the viewport does not do it for a play-in-editor window without focus.
  - Music and atmosphere: four flat stereo layers on the generator (`TickMusic`: sets `Music.Explore`, `Music.Combat`, `Atmos.Explore`, `Atmos.Combat`). A fight is on while a monster within `CombatMusicDistance` is after her or blows fell a second ago; the battle pair fades in from the track's start (each fight the next track), and `CombatMusicHold` seconds after the fight the wandering pair fades back in where it was (the generator keeps each layer's position itself: a faded-out component starts again from 0). Which tracks a session gets comes from the session seed. Her breath loops were removed by the user's wish: of her moving only the footsteps are heard (her cry when hurt or dying and her effort on a swing stay: the user confirmed it). The sound self test plays no music, only the room tone (it measures single sounds; with nothing at all playing the engine does not process the silent submix, and a submix recording then has no audio for the silent stretches: it came out seconds shorter than the test).
  - Nobody hears a test: counting `PlaySound` calls proved nothing about what comes out. `799_rdc_sound_test.ps1` (the game with `-RDSoundTest=<dir>`) records the device's output with sounds at known places and `rdc_sound_check.py` measures it: do that after touching attenuation or spatialisation. A play-in-editor or game window without focus is silent (`FApp::SetUnfocusedVolumeMultiplier`; the test sets it).
  - Movie Render Queue sound: the job needs a `MoviePipelineWaveOutput` setting and the editor started with `-deterministicaudio`. That non-realtime audio device loses every *spatialised* source (measured with test sounds from all sides: only flat and non-spatialised ones are in the `.wav`), so under `-deterministicaudio` the generator plays its placed sounds with the distance falloff but without direction (`bSpatializeSounds`, `AreSoundsSpatialized`). Run tests and normal play in an editor started without the switch.
  - A monster asked the door's *progress* before pushing it (`GetDoorOpenAlpha < 0.01`): at more than about 140 ticks a second (the render queue's sub-steps, a fast machine) the leaf had not moved that far by the next tick and the monster toggled it for ever. Ask for the state a thing is heading to (`IsTargetOpen`), not how far it has got.
