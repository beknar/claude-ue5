# random_dungeonmagic_level4 and random_dungeonmagic_level5 (the choice of hero, the interface, the Summoner and her faces, armour, weapons, tiers)

<!-- moved from CLAUDE.md (2026-10-23) to load only when these files are opened -->

## The heroes' properties (the user's, 2026-10-23)

- Warrior: wields all weapons, wears all armour (light, medium, heavy), +30 % melee damage, the highest hit points (140).
- Mage: casts and learns all spells (fire, dark fire), the lowest hit points (70); no armour, none of the table's weapons.
- Warlock: wields all weapons, wears only light and medium armour, casts and learns a limited set of spells (fire), balanced hit points (100).
- Rogue: wields daggers and ranged weapons (+30 % ranged damage), wears only light armour, uncanny dodge (B), balanced hit points (100).
- The Summoner (level 5's fifth hero) wears all armour, wields none of the table's weapons, casts the Mage's spells.
- The cards (`SourceArt/random_dungeon_magic4/heroes.json`, `random_dungeon_magic5/summoner_hero.json`), the game's rules (`FRDHeroDef::Wears`, `weapons_table.json`'s `who`) and README's tables must say the same. "Learns" spells is on the cards only: no spell is found in the dungeon.

## Armour by weight (`856`)

- A set goes on only if its weight (`armour.json`'s `weight` -> `FRDItemDef::ArmourWeight`) is in the hero's `Wears` and a fit was made for her mesh (`FRDItemDef::Fits`); else `ARDDungeonGenerator::ArmourRefusal` says why (the message and the inventory's text).
- A new set or hero: fit it (`849_rdm5_armour_export.py heroes <mesh>`, `blender -b -P summoner3_armour.py -- <SourceArt/summoner3> --hero <mesh> [--pct N] [set ...]`, `851_rdm5_armour_import.py <mesh> <set> ...`), then `856`. Look at `armour_fit/<mesh>/<set>_front|side.png` before importing.
- `--pct`: the share of her skin's distance from each bone the fit stays outside. 95 (the default, and the Summoner's) caught straps and holsters on the Warrior's thigh and ballooned the bear warrior's trousers: the Warrior's and the Warlock's bear warrior's sets are fitted at 85, the Rogue's tiefling's at 70, the others at 95. The tiefling's greaves are plates strapped to the shin: they stand a few cm proud on a slim leg whatever the percentile.
- `853`'s `heroarmour` step tries every set on each hero (worn with her own fit and following her bones, or refused with the reason).

## The armour table, loot and crafting (`857`)

- The data: `summoner3/armour.json` (the user's 27 sets: type, damage reduction and speed ratings, durability, tier; the weight they give: full plate and heavy heavy, medium and partial medium, light light, orc by speed rating) and `random_dungeon_magic5/loot_table.json` (the potions' and foods' tiers, 15 materials, 5 repair items, the recipes). `857` writes them into the level; `856`'s rule (a fit for every hero who wears the weight) is applied there too.
- A Fab piece's FBX carries only the bones that piece is skinned to (MysticDungeon's arm piece has no feet, its torso no calves): `summoner3_armour.py` builds each set's source rig from all its pieces and faces it by the clavicles when there are no feet. A bone frame aimed at a missing child came out identity: the fit then ran but put the piece wrong.
- Big trousers ballooned on the slim heroes (the orc raider's, the crocodile warrior's, the epic plate's) though no scale was clipped: 90 % of a bone's armour vertices outside her skin was decided by straps, belts and pouch backs near the bone. `--legs 60` takes 60 % for the hips, thighs and calves and pushes the rest out to her skin; 60 on the torso let her chest through a breastplate.
- Armour is carried once: a set found again mends the one she has to full (one durability per set name). Blows that reach her wear the worn set (`WearDownArmour`, after the share is taken: the blow that empties it still gets the whole share); at 0 it takes half.
- Anvils are furniture pieces (`AnvilPiece`) recorded where they are placed (`AddPiece`, also in play for the saved start section, whose furniture is not built again). `EnsureAnvil` adds one in the great hall and in a section that has none, drawing no random stream; its first rule (8 free neighbours, two tiles off the lanes) found no spot in the great hall, whose quadrant corners are furniture: a free tile off the lanes with its 4 neighbours free. The start section must be built again (`857`'s `build_start_section`) for its anvil to be saved.
- F at an anvil opens the crafting panel (the generator's `InteractNearest`: an anvil nearer than any door or container). The panel and the inventory share the pause; F also closes it, and the generator's own F in the same frame must not open it again (`CraftClosedFrame`).
- Nobody hears a test, and the editor mutes play when it is not in front (`UEditorEngine` sets the volume to 0 unless `ULevelEditorMiscSettings::bAllowBackgroundAudio`): `853`'s `fire` step sets that for itself (`unreal.find_object(None, "/Script/UnrealEd.Default__LevelEditorMiscSettings")`, property `bAllowBackgroundAudio` by its C++ name: the Python name is not found) and records the output through the generator (`StartRecordingOutput` / `StopRecordingOutput`: Python has no `AudioMixerBlueprintLibrary` here). Without it the recordings were all zeros. A fire's voice picks one of its set's loop files at random: before and after compared two files and measured -9.5 and -13.2 dB for a change of -8; the step holds each set to its first file (-8.68, -8.63, -9.12 in three runs: the room's other sounds still vary).
- The reel (`815 ... craft`): in play the crafting panel and the inventory pause the game, in a render the world goes on: from the crafting to the jump the monsters are made to leave her be (the "quiet" event, said by a caption). The pilot's `threat()` takes on any monster it can see within 15 m: on a walk to a point only one coming at her is fought (`to_point`), or a dog nearby made four fights of the walk to the anvil.

## Reels

- A reel may lure monsters to her (`815`'s `lure()`: `AggroDistance` 45 m for her fights; the great hall's nearest are 35-57 m off by the way, and without it she ran empty hallways) only with a caption that says so (`rdm_reel_overlay.py`'s `lured`).

## The lessons of levels 4 and 5

  - random_dungeonmagic_level4 (`824`-`828`): the four heroes are data on the generator (`Heroes`, from `heroes.json`; their weapons copied as text from the generators of the levels they come from, so they are those levels' own). When play begins `ApplyHero` writes the chosen one into the generator's own fields (`Weapons`, `PlayerMaxHealth`, controls, roll...) before anything reads them; `ARDHeroGameMode` spawns her pawn by asking the generator (the level's actors exist when the player is spawned). The menu level opens the dungeon with `?Hero=<name>`; from the editor the dungeon plays `DefaultHero`; tests change hero in play with `SwitchHero`.
  - `797` rewrites the open level's whole `Sounds` table from `sounds.json`: run `825` after it on level 4 (it adds the dark fire's sets as copies of the fire's).
  - Level 4's reel: a Movie Render Queue job renders one level, and the menu opens another: the menu is rendered on its own with START staying put (`ARDHeroSelect.bStayOnConfirm`), each hero's run on its own (the pilot puts her in the level's place with `SwitchHero` while the render warms up), and `rdm4_reel_assemble.py` cuts them together. The open inventory pauses the game, and the queue renders a world that goes on: the reel turns that off (`bInventoryPausesGame`). The queue draws no HUD: the menu's card and the inventory panel are drawn over the frames from the pilots' records.
  - Level 4's interface art comes from the user's InvokeAI server on the LAN (`ui_art.json`'s `invoke.host`; `rdm4_ui_gen.py`, `rdm4_ui_cut.py`). FLUX has no negative prompt and paints what a prompt names, "no" or not: "no cross symbol" put a red cross (an emblem games are asked not to use) on all 16 healing potions, "no signature" scribbled signatures, "no frame, no border" painted torn-paper backdrops, "no plate" plates, and "healing" alone invites the cross. Write prompts in positive terms. The frames' iron and gold in the common style put the bread in an iron tin: each kind has its own style. FLUX's "white" ground is not flat (a vignette darkens the corners to 190), so the cut compares each pixel with the ground around it; white icing went with a white ground (the sweet roll is painted on teal). The cut's numbers catch backdrops and cut-off shapes; a symbol, a signature joined to the shape or a plate only the eye catches: look at every contact sheet. Don't use a Fab pack's pictures as references for an image model: the UI pack looked at for this says "Allows usage with AI: No". The fills (a bar's liquid) got the white backdrop too and came out as brush strokes on white: a piece that fills its picture has no backdrop. White enclosed by a shape (between a crossbow's bow and string) is out of the border's reach: `enclosed`. A board's corner ornaments reach further in than its frame: titles at a board's corners are indented. The divider is fitted, not stretched (a stretch smeared its gem). The HUD (`FRDUIDraw`) and the reel's overlay (`rdm4_ui_draw.py`) draw the pieces by the same rules and sizes: change both (the big map too: `DrawPaintedMap` / `painted_map`). Pillow's ImageDraw on an RGBA image replaces the pixels instead of blending: see-through tiles or boxes drawn over a painted board cut holes in it (the game showed through the big map's board); draw them on a layer of their own and composite it. See-through tiles drawn edge to edge with a half-pixel overlap show a grid: snap them to whole pixels. `rdm4_ui_gen.py` writes its manifest in the out_dir it is given and the export takes every pick from that folder: a new element goes into the folder of the earlier batches (a run into an empty folder exported a `layout.json` with the one new piece).
  - Level 4's lettering (`rdm4_fonts.py`, `830`'s `UIFonts`; `FRDUIDraw::Text` with a role: title Cinzel, text Alegreya, label Alegreya SC). Canvas draws a runtime-cached `UFont` with its glyphs rendered at `LegacyFontSize` and then scaled: the HUD makes one font per role and size in play (`FontCache`, a UPROPERTY) so that nothing is drawn scaled up from small glyphs. The engine's Roboto is 10 points; Alegreya's capitals start lower in its line than Roboto's (0.369 em against 0.217): drawn where the Roboto layouts put text it sat on the boards' frames and out of its slots, so each face has a `Lift` and the height reported is Roboto's line. The repository's Google font files are variable fonts (Unreal and Pillow draw their default weight): `rdm4_fonts.py` takes static files from the CSS API. Cinzel has no ▲. A property named `Raise` would be Python's keyword `raise`: name properties so they stay plain words. Building a `UFont` in C++ needs `SlateCore` in the module's dependencies (`FFontData` links from there). The reel's overlay and the assembler draw with the same files and roles (`sc()` writes a label as the HUD does).
  - random_dungeonmagic_level5, the Summoner (`832`-`836`; `summoner_rig.py`, `summoner_clips.py`, `summoner_face.py`). Her GLB's rig has generic bone names and no clips: the bones are renamed to the mannequin's and the Mage's clips retargeted in Blender by direction (each bone pointing where the Mage's points, turned about it as hers turns). Unreal's IK retargeter, from the pack's mannequin onto her rig with its extra bones (a fourth spine bone, a palm, a fourth finger joint), folded her up on the floor in the death clip; the Mage's flinch and death are exported as FBX (`832`) and transferred the same way. Blender's FBX import sets the scene to the file's 30 fps: left so, the build keyed every clip at 30 and they came out 0.8 times their length (she walked a quarter faster): restore the rate and sample at the build's. Her feet turn as the Mage's do from their rest (she stands in heels; aimed along the Mage's flat boot her shoe tore apart). The finger order is measured: index to pinky from where the thumb ends, not where it starts. Her face: the eyes and mouth are painted; the face's skin, each eye, the lashes and the brows are separate mesh pieces (weld the seams to see them), the bangs too. Patches painted in the texture's space would split an eye across two islands: they are painted on a front view and baked back through that projection, on the face's front pieces only (the eye sockets' steep rims included, or the old eyeliner showed as a ring; the lashes' pieces included, or the old lashes stayed over the closed eyes), the covered skin filled with the skin round it blurred in. Pillow's way of blending into an empty canvas ringed every patch (blend 'over', colour kept straight). A property named `In` is Python's keyword: `FadeIn` / `FadeOut`. The face test measures pictures, not weights: the idle and the torches' flicker changed two pictures at rest by 3 %, so it stops her idle and captures the unlit colour (`SCS_BASE_COLOR`). In the face reel a camera keyed where the death clip alone puts her head, and one attached to her head bone, both saw only floor in the render: the death face is shot as her eyes shut, before the fall.
  - Her MetaHuman face (`837`-`841`, `rdm5_mh_face.py`, `summoner_mh_prep.py`, C++ `FRDMetaFace` / `URDMetaFaceAnimInstance` in `RD/RDFace`). The MetaHuman conform (`conform_to_target_meshes`, `HEAD_ONLY` needs `pipeline_name` `head_only`: an empty name fails with "Failed to find pipeline") fitted to her anime head made a grotesque (her skull is twice a face's height) and fitted to her face alone a lopsided flat face; MetaHuman's face tracker found her painted eyelids, not her mouth. The face is a preset (Aera) instead; the cloud steps (`request_texture_sources`, `request_auto_rigging`) worked with the editor's existing sign-in. Epic's `ABP_Face_PostProcess` copies the body's bones by name from the mesh the face is attached to before RigLogic: attached to her (other axes) it folded the face's neck to her waist; `838 postprocess` makes a copy without that node. Her face is cut away by a mask in her texture's UV space: a texel a hidden and a kept face share must go to the hidden one (kept, it left slivers of her old face over the MetaHuman's lip); the mask with no mip levels and never-stream made the masked material cut her whole body away; the material needs `used_with_morph_targets` (her mesh keeps the old face's shapes: without it the game drew her with the default material, in the editor she looked right). The anime bangs' tips crossed the eyes and brows as flat strips: cut at brow height.
  - Summoner2 (`842`-`848`, `summoner2_prep.py`, `summoner2_outfit.py`, `s2_underwear.py`): a whole MetaHuman fitted to a realistic Tripo model. `conform_to_target_meshes` with `auto_solve` on picks its own solver settings and ignores `body_conform_solve_settings` (identical coefficients whatever was set): overrides need `auto_solve` off. A HEAD_ONLY pass after the COMBINED one shrank the head and moved it 10 cm; with no skull in the target the head collapsed: the cranium under the hair is an ellipsoid. Fitted in her T-pose and set upright in the A-pose, her bowed head sits 2.7 cm higher: compare face shapes with the head's place taken out (MetaHuman's face tracker on both pictures). Tripo's hair is fused to the face: the fringe stands out of the forehead and the side locks lie on the cheeks as strips (they widened the jaw); a ray test finds them. Groom wardrobe parameters only exist on the preview collection (`get_preview_collection`, `on_edit_preview_collection`); the grooms' colours are material instances given to the built grooms' slots, one per slot type (MI_Hair, _Cards, _Helmet: the strands' material on the cards slots drew blotches). Ombre on: `hairMelanin` colours the roots, the `Ombre*` parameters the lengths, `OmbreShift` where they start. Editor captures show a MetaHuman at the LOD the viewport camera gives it (helmet hair, a coarse face): the face component holds the grooms at LOD 0 (`SetForcedLOD`). MetaHuman bakes grey briefs into the body's textures (only the top has a switch). The posed DNA's mesh cannot be read back (`get_mesh_for_body_conforming_from_dna`: INVALID_INPUT_DATA). `delete_asset` of a MetaHuman Character fails while it is open for editing or its preview stands: try characters under names of their own. A batch retarget into a folder that has its outputs adds `_S3`, `_S4` copies: empty it first. The face test's pictures with strand hair: show the face and body meshes only.
  - The level now (`849`-`853`): a Save As copy of level 4 (the Summoner2 level's `.umap` set aside) with Summoner3 as a fifth hero and armour (`ERDItemKind::Armour`, `FRDArmourFit`: a hero's fitted meshes; `WearArmour` puts them on her with her body as leader pose). Everything level 4 has (menu `random_dungeonmagic_level5_select` by `826 5`, `rd.HeroMenu`, `830 --level 5`) applies; its tests are `853` through `828_rdm4.ps1 ... -lv 5`.
    - The sets are refitted in Blender (`summoner3_armour.py`) bone by bone: each bone its own frame (a leaf keeps its own head with its parent's axes: sharing the parent's frame stretched the helmet with the neck), the pieces written in world coordinates with no parent (left parented under an armature of another scale they went missing in the renders).
    - A map property read from one level's actor (`get_editor_property` on a `TMap`) points into that actor: set on another level's actor after a map load, it hung the editor (one core spinning, no dialog). Copy such maps as text (`export_text` / `import_text`).
    - Interchange will not import a skeletal mesh while play-in-editor runs ("Cannot import SkeletalMeshNode asset at runtime"): stop play first.
    - A wardrobe's two doors are one container: opening the first opens the second, and a test that toggled every index in one frame shut both again (found nothing). Skip one whose target is open (`ARDSection::IsTargetOpen`).
    - Whether worn armour follows her: "the nearest body vertex keeps its distance" failed on a plate skirt (its nearest vertex was on the other leg as she ran). `MeasureArmourFollow` measures vertices bound to one bone against where her bone of that name puts them (0.09 cm standing, 0.48 running at 3.4 m/s; 1.3 m if the set stayed in its pose).
    - The FOUND plaque's picture counts in the HUD's drawn art: a check of the inventory's pictures waits for it to go. `RestartPlay` empties her inventory and takes her armour off (`ClearItems`).
    - The great hall's tile path went 27 m round a corner between two chests 6.5 m apart: the reel's pilot (`815` with level 5) heads for the farthest route point a body-sized sweep shows clear.
    - The earlier UI art batch folder was gone (scratch): `rdm4_ui_cut.py --export <dir> --merge` adds a new batch's picks to the exported `layout.json` and keeps the rest. `830` runs in the editor's working directory: give it absolute paths.
    - The weapons table and the tiers (`854`, `855`; `weapons_table.json`): the generator's `MaxTier` (0 on every other level: nothing changes there) makes a section's tier its ring round the start plus one; a tier past 2 is generated only once every section of the tier before is cleared (`Progress`: its rooms explored, its chests and trunks looted, every monster it spawned killed). A section generated again skips the monsters she killed by their place in its spawning order (`SpawnIndex`): the skipped ones still draw their randoms, or the others would move. Hallways into a shut tier get a grating, out of the last tier a wall (`ARDSection::UpdateSeals`, rebuilt for every section when a tier opens). `bLootAllSections` puts items in every section's containers, an item's `Tiers` says where.
    - Found weapons join the generator's `Weapons` in play (one per hero who can wield it: the item's `WeaponFits`); the combat component keeps their `Wear` beside them. A weapon that breaks is removed at the start of the next tick, the last one first: a bolt of it may still be flying, and indices shift. Only the hero's own weapons have holster components.
    - A weapon's grip is measured on its mesh (`854`): slices along its long axis, 1 cm each. A slice with no vertex is unknown, not narrow (long faces run through it). A pommel or an end knob can be wider than the handle, and a katana's tsuba or a sabre's crossbar is 1 cm thick: the guard is the first wide slice once 5 cm of handle have gone by. The blade's end is the one that narrows to a point (a curved sabre's blade end measured as round as its handle). The Medieval crossbow's widest part is half way down its stock (the prod sweeps back; its front is the spike end, -X): the crossbows' ways are given, not found.
