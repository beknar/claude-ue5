# The magic levels 1 to 3 (casters, the Rogue, the tactical maps, clips made in Blender, the Warlock's hair)

<!-- moved from CLAUDE.md (2026-10-23) to load only when these files are opened -->

- **random_dungeonmagic_level / random_dungeonmagic_level2 / random_dungeonmagic_level3 / random_dungeonmagic_level4** (`810`-`828`): Save As copies down a chain (combat -> magic -> magic2 -> magic3 -> magic4) with their own game modes (`BP_RDM_GameMode`, `BP_RDM2_GameMode`: the casters as pawns). A spell is a weapon with `bSpell` (a bolt instead of a blow, its effects, light and sounds as data); `bAlongside` makes it a spell cast with its own keys (right mouse, E) while the drawn weapon stays in her hand. The combat level has neither, so everything new stays behind those flags; after touching `URDCombatComponent` run 793 on the combat level, 813 on the magic levels 1 to 3 and 828 on level 4.
  - Judge an effect where it plays, from the player's camera, at the level's own exposure. A capture with the exposure pushed up 9 stops made DarkHalls' `NS_FireEye` look like a fireball; in the dungeon it was a few embers, and the first bolts flew unseen while the room lit up orange.
  - Niagara sprite sizes here ignore the component's scale (`NS_FirePool` is about a metre at 0.1 and at 0.35): to make an effect smaller, use another effect.
  - A system that builds up over a second never shows on a bolt that flies for half a second: `810` makes copies with `warmup_time` (`NS_RDM_*_Warm`); the pack's own are left alone.
  - Python cannot build an `FInputActionValue` (its fields are not properties), and in PIE `query_keys_mapped_to_action` returns nothing and `has_mapping_context` says False for the context that moves her. Tests press keys with `URDCombatComponent.SimulateKey` (her player controller's `InputKey`): the whole way from the key to the movement and the spell is then measured.
  - The tactical maps (`bTacticalMaps`, all six dungeon levels by `821`; `ARDHUD`): the minimap's feathered rim is a soft disc texture made in play and the terrain is drawn tile by tile with an alpha that falls off to the rim; the big map is drawn from the same tile set (the map data's rectangles, cached until they change) as runs of edges where a discovered floor tile meets no tile. While it is open the game goes on: the combat component takes input and the HUD's panels and prompts stay. The arrow keys (D-pad) scroll it (`MapPan`), C (R3) centres it; the project's `IMC_Default` maps the arrows to `IA_Move` too (scrolling walked her), so while the map is open the generator adds an input context of its own at priority 1000 whose action consumes the four arrows, and the D-pad's weapon choice waits. Markers: elite = more than 1.25 x its tier's average hit points (the trolls); gold stars on chests and trunks not looted yet (the storerooms' barrels and crates buried the map; a star is the loot, not the lid: `ARDSection::IsContainerLooted`, kept by the generator in `LootedContainers` so a section dropped and generated again stays looted; `RestartPlay` empties it); an exit diamond where the next section has nothing discovered (a section is generated as she nears it, so 'not generated' was hardly ever seen). 792's `map` step checks she walks and attacks with it open.
  - random_dungeonmagic_level3, the Rogue (`822`, `823`; `ai models/Rogue.glb`, tag `Rogue`): the generator's `bRogueControls` (1 / 2, the wheel or Y switch weapons, the left mouse or RB uses the one drawn, B rolls). The crossbow is a spell weapon whose bolt is a mesh (`BoltMesh`, shot from `BoltMuzzle` on the weapon, the next one lying on it, stuck in a wall `BoltStickSeconds`, `BoltTurnRate` 0: straight). A weapon not drawn is carried on her (`HolsterBone` / `HolsterGrip`). The roll: her clip in place on the slot while a root motion source (constant force, `IgnoreZAccumulate`) carries her and her move input is ignored; a way behind her is a backward roll; blows miss her within `RollDodge` (shares of the clip).
  - In Unreal's Python `-v` on an `unreal.Vector` negates `v` in place and returns the same object: `make_rot_from_xz(-front, -back)` turned `back` around, put the crossbow holster in front of her and the thigh daggers 32 cm too high. Write `v * -1.0`.
  - A strike that winds up fast (a backhand, a cut from above) is fastest in its wind-up: 822 measures the blow after the wind-up key that `rogue_clips.py` writes to `times.json`, and the crossbow's release (no hand speed marks it) is taken from there too.
  - Dark Fantasy Weapons' crossbow clips are additive aim-offset poses for the pack's own AnimBP, not clips to play: the shot, the dagger strikes and the roll were made in Blender. Its arrow trail spawns a point every 2.5 m at 13 % alpha: on a 45 m/s bolt it showed nothing, so 822 tunes a copy (3 cm, 0.1 s, a point every 20 cm and one where it starts: `USSFXLibrary.set_spawn_per_unit`). Look at a fast projectile with the game slowed (`set_global_time_dilation`): at full speed it crosses a room in a few frames.
  - `tripo_build.py` given a relative output folder wrote the meshes but not the texture: give it absolute paths.
  - Her hands were bent the wrong way in every weapon clip (the user saw it in the quartet reel), and nothing had measured a wrist. The clips aimed the blade along her thumb, whatever it cost the wrist: the crossbow hand ended up folded back 150 degrees against the forearm, the dagger wrists bent 50 to 90 degrees sideways or twisted half round, and the forearm's twist bone never moved. `rogue_clips.py` now solves each arm: the elbow is a hinge, the wrist stays inside its limits, half the twist goes to `lowerarm_twist_01`, and the blade leans toward the fingers (`GRIP_TILT`, which 822 reads from `times.json`). It prints the worst wrist ("ROGUE wrists"). The wrist's axes come from the knuckles (index to little finger): the thumb bone stands half way off the palm, and axes taken from it were 48 degrees from square, so the limits did not hold. Her cape is skinned to her upper arms: once the elbows were real hinges, the upper arms turned 120 to 160 degrees about themselves and lifted the cape off her back (it showed in the reel). The turn is now held to `ARM_ROLL` (40 degrees): a held arm's forearm may swing about the upper arm's axis instead, and the crossbow's elbow is placed accordingly. Look at the clips from behind, not only at the hands.
  - Re-running `620` for a character deletes `BP_JRPG<CH>Character` and makes it again: the editor hands its dungeon child to `BP_ThirdPersonCharacter`, and the child's `MoveScale` then stops compiling. `822` puts the child back on its parent; `819` makes `MoveScale` an override again. `610`'s Idle clip swap also has to handle `Sequence=None` (a re-run's retargeted AnimBP came with no Idle clip).
  - Their controls are a layout of their own (generator `bCasterControls`; the combat level keeps its X / RT attack and Y / LT spell): RB the weapon in hand, LB the spell, LT or R switch walking / running, X opens and Back (View) opens the big map (in the generator, beside F and M). The switch is the casters' Blueprint variable `RunMode` (`819`), which `MoveScale` reads and the combat component sets by name. The HUD's upper-left panel lists `ControlsKeys` or `ControlsPad` by the device used last (`IsUsingGamepad`). Tests push sticks with `SimulateAxis` (an `IE_Axis` event: the enhanced input's `Gamepad_Left2D` / `Right2D` follow it).
  - After a Blueprint compile from Python, a class default object fetched before it is a `REINST_` copy without the new variables: fetch it again.
  - A monster's hit points and damage follow the player's hit points and average blow: with heroes to choose, the Warrior's 140 hit points and the Mage's 70 would have been cancelled by the monsters. `MonsterReferenceHealth` / `MonsterReferenceBlow` fix them.
  - Her inventory pauses the game (`SetGamePaused`). The generator ticks while paused (`bTickEvenWhenPaused`) and then works only the inventory; the game's clock stands still, so a test that waits on game time while the inventory is open never returns: wait on real time.
  - The menu opens the dungeon a tick after Enter and its world is gone with it: a test that let go of the key through the menu's actor failed with "ObjectInstance is null". Read what the menu did each tick until it disappears.
  - `819` looks for the `MoveScale` to override up the whole parent chain: the Warrior's dungeon character is a child of the city level's, whose parent has it.
  - A step that measures her hit points or her running speed sets the monsters' `AggroDistance` to 0 for its duration (blows spoiled the food and regeneration readings) and runs her along a line a capsule sweep found clear (the great hall's pillars cut a measured run 9 % short). Every container of the start section counts for the loot (1015 with the storerooms' barrels and crates).
  - The Warlock's hair (`tripo_hair.py`, `820`): Tripo's rig has no hair bones and its hair, skin, leather and cape are one mesh with one texture. Hair is found by position boxes plus colour (warm brown; red, bright skin and cool leather left out; near-black undecided, smoothed over neighbours); shadowed skin passed as hair until her chest was cut out of the front locks' box. Check the weights on the texture in a close render before importing.
  - Importing a mesh onto an existing skeleton adds new bones but keeps the skeleton's old reference pose for bones it already had: after the hair chains changed, `get_reference_pose(skeleton)` gave the old ones (a lock's bodies were set up for bones twice as long). Read rest places from the mesh (a component without animation), as `820` does, and write them for the tests (`hair_ref.json`).
  - AnimDynamics from Python: a node's settings are a struct imported as text in parentheses (without them nothing is taken, silently). An AnimBP deleted while loaded and made again comes back without graphs; rebuilt in place, its Input Pose node must be kept (one made again passed on the reference pose: she stood in her T-pose). World space left the bodies behind as she ran; component space with a share of her acceleration / velocity / turning does not. Spheres pushed each strand out a different way and tore them apart; one plane under the back hair pushes them alike. With 4 / 1 solver iterations the joints came apart by 60 % and more; 48 / 16 holds them to about 35 % for a moment.
  - The editor minimized runs play at 3 fps (everything slows, not only rendering); `bThrottleCPUWhenNotForeground` does not cover it. Check the window (`IsIconic`) before blaming a feature for being slow.
  - Clips made in Blender (`caster_clips.py`, `darkcaster_clips.py`): turning a hand about its own axis only moves the thumb (the blade) within the plane across the fingers; a blade pointed nearly along the fingers missed by up to 77 degrees. `darkcaster_clips.py`'s `blade` operation sets the blade exactly and the fingers across it; its preview prints every pose's blade direction against the one wanted.
  - The reel pilot: `RestartPlay` puts the monsters back over several frames and the old ones still count as alive for a frame or two, so a choice made at once saw nobody; it waits half a second and counts. Monsters wander, so the start is chosen by what stays put (a chest by the way east) and the monster is hunted from there. A chest's map point can be across a wall from where its path ends: the pilot walks to a stand point from which F names that chest.
