also follow this guide:

Project Setup

• Enable required plugins: Ensure Unreal Engine's PCG (Procedural Content Generation) plugin is enabled for floor and roof dressing workflows.
• Study the example map: Open the included large example cave map to see how modular meshes, combined meshes, and Blueprint pieces are connected and dressed.

2. Route Tunnels with Splines

• Create spline tunnels: Place spline-driven tunnel actors and choose size variants (small, medium, or large).
• Shape the layout: Adjust spline points in the editor to shape the tunnel path. Note that the system relies on manual routing rather than automatic tunnel generation.
• Optimize editing performance: Temporarily disable collision while editing splines, then re-enable it afterward to regenerate collision.

3. Connect Rooms, Caps, and Stairs

• Attach rooms and caps: Use the spline and room Blueprint details panels to spawn small, medium, or large rooms at spline ends, or attach caps to close off openings.
• Align connection points: Select specific connection points on Blueprint room actors so tunnels and rooms align cleanly, adjusting yaw offset manually if needed.
• Add transitions and intersections: Use room intersection controls to insert stairs, connect additional splines from room connection points, or place combined-mesh prefabs for faster layout.

4. Dress with PCG and Lighting

• Populate floor and roof detail: Use PCG components to scatter rocks, foliage, shrubs, and debris across floors and ceilings.
• Tune PCG parameters: Adjust settings such as spawn density/frequency, offset height, ratios, and amounts to fit your cave's look.
• Place lighting helpers: Use the toolkit's light and atmosphere placement helpers to spawn or adjust wall/roof lights and light shafts

implement the following for cave environments:

Crafting materials (anvil, alchemist's and librarian's tables, oven)

- Deepiron ore veins in the cave walls. A cave-only metal for a set of anvil recipes, one step above what a town smith sells at the region's tier.
- Glowcap and grave-moss, fungi for potions: darksight (you see farther without a torch), poison resist, a slow regeneration potion.
- Cave-pearl, blind-fish oil: the oil fuels torches and the ankh for longer, which matters because both burn down.
- Bat-wing leather, crawler chitin: light armour patches, and chitin as a repair item for medium armour.
- Crystal geode shards, cut at the librarian's table into scroll inks or socket stones; a colour per region.
- Bones of something old (a giant's femur, a wyrm's vertebra): rare, for a named weapon recipe.
- Petrified wood or amber with something inside: an alchemy reagent, and also a collectible (below).
- Regional ores tied to the climate phase: frost quartz in cold regions (warm-gear upgrades) and sun-glass in hot ones (cool-gear upgrades).

Weapons

- The miner's pick: a two-handed weapon that also breaks cave rubble, opening side tunnels and hidden caches.
- A shard blade of cave crystal: high damage, low durability, mended only with geode shards.
- A lost adventurer's weapon: a named blade on a skeleton deep in the cave, with a note telling who carried it. Each cave picks one from a pool.
- Bone weapons (a jawbone club, a rib dagger): dark, cheap and brittle.
- A lightless bow whose arrows don't show where you shot from, which suits the Rogue's stealth.

Armour

- Spelunker's light armour: quieter footsteps, a little darksight.
- Chitin plate: heavy, with poison resistance.
- The drowned knight's set, from a flooded cave chamber, one piece in each cave of a region; wearing the whole set lets you breathe longer under water.
- Deepiron versions of existing sets: the same looks, a better tier.

Rings, charms and other wearables (alongside the twelve named roamers' rings)

- Ring of the Echo: shows nearby monsters on the minimap as dim pings.
- Glow-worm amulet: a faint light without a torch, leaving both hands free.
- The miner's lucky stone: more ore from each vein.
- Cursed relics: strong but with a cost, such as more damage while you bleed slowly, or more gold while monsters notice you sooner. A temple could lift the curse for a price.

Lasting rewards (as with the collectibles and the temple's trade)

- Fossils and relics, a collectible set per region; a full set traded at the temple gives lasting hit points or mana, as the existing collectibles do.
- Ancient tablets that teach a recipe or a spell, using the existing recipe-item system.
- Cave paintings: you find them, and they mark the region's other caves and secrets on the big map.
- Angel Tokens, guaranteed from a cave's last boss.

Things that link back to the open world

- Treasure maps pointing at a ruin or a structure on the surface.
- A contract target's lair: a contract beast that retreats underground, so you finish the contract in its cave.
- A prisoner or lost traveller found alive deep inside, who becomes a quest or settles a reclaimed camp.
- A waystone fragment that lets you fast-travel to that cave's mouth.
- The tower's key: the cave's deepest room holds the key to the broken tower's top, with a lookout and a chest up there.

Gold and trade goods

- Raw gems and old coins of a dead kingdom, which merchants buy, and which a collector in one city pays double for.
- Dragonglass or blackstone that only one city's smith works, giving you a reason to travel there.
