# Capabilities

[README](../README.md) · [Levels](../README.md#levels) · [Documentation](../README.md#documentation)

What the project does today. Everything here is built by the scripts in this repository and can be rebuilt from the local packs; the [Levels](../README.md#levels) table links each level's document, which has the detail.

**Characters and animation**
- A MetaHuman conformed to a 3D scan (`fig4`): face and body fitted to the scan, a strand hair groom, the scanned outfit refitted to her, studio lighting.
- Characters from six kinds of source on the same third-person controls: the MetaHuman, a Meshy AI biped with 18 moves of its own, a Unity-format Fab soldier, a static Tripo GLB rigged in Blender, animated Tripo GLBs playing their own clips, and Meshy JRPG characters (one of them an unrigged mesh whose skeleton is worked out from its shape).
- IK rigs and retargeters built from Python: UE5 mannequin and soldier animation onto the other characters, three Fab weapon animation packs onto the player of the dungeon.
- Feet that stay put: walk and run speeds measured from the stance foot, clips grounded on the sole contacts, root locking, foot IK on uneven ground.
- Clips made in Blender for characters that lack them: an idle, spell casts and sword cuts posed on the character's own rig by where each bone points, which way the palm faces and which way the blade points, eased into each other (`blender_numpy/caster_clips.py`, `darkcaster_clips.py`); the moment a blow lands or a bolt leaves is measured on the imported clip.
- The dungeon's heroes (levels 4 and 5, Mistward Castle): the Warrior (Warrior3 on level 5 and in the castle, with her own two-handed slash and the old Warrior's clips retargeted for her other weapons), the Mage, the Warlock (her hair swinging on AnimDynamics), the Rogue, on level 5 the Summoner (Summoner3), and in the castle the Martial Artist (MartialArtist2) and its own Summoner (Summoner4). They are Tripo models rigged and given clips in Blender, their arms lengthened and shoulders widened so that two hands meet on a hilt. The 27 armour sets are refitted to each hero's body bone by bone. The 29 found weapons are held by grips measured on their meshes. The Warrior carries 22 shields on her forearm, and any hero carries a torch in her left hand.
- Monsters: the gallery's 55 usable kinds, placed by tier. Each stands on the floor as measured in play (the lowest point of its drawn body), walks at a speed measured from its own feet, and from tier 3 poisons, weakens or slows. Bosses are 1.35 times bigger. In the castle, prisoners from the NPC Prisoners pack walk out of their cells, and caged monsters unlock their cells and come out. The cities' dungeons add eleven kinds each among the castle's: The War Engine's machines and half-machines, the sewers' drowned sailors and fish-men (those on the UE4 mannequin rig with no fighting clips of their own play the Fantasy Pack's; all 22 measured playing their clips).
- Townsfolk and animals: the NPC packs' townspeople on their own bodies with heads, hair and beards worn as parts, walking as far as their feet move; their work clips made in Blender on the packs' mannequin (stirring, turning a spit, tending, scattering feed, sitting to drink and talk, carrying a tray, talking); the farm animals made in Blender (hen, rabbit, goat, pig and cow, two or three colourings each, an idle, a graze and a walk whose stride was measured on the planted feet: the packs, the library and the Fab vault had none); the wilderness's deer, wolves and squirrels from Wildlands, and the farm's rabbit as its hare. Aurorim's four wings given bones and beats in Blender (`aurorim_build.py`).

**Worlds**
- Hand-built from pack maps: a meadow (procedural Nanite terrain, rocks, grass), a photo studio, a medieval village and a castle with a lake village whose houses were opened up and furnished by purpose, a sci-fi city joined to a megastructure with furnished rooms behind automatic doors.
- Procedural, in C++ (`ARTTownGenerator`, `ARDDungeonGenerator`, `AIVVillageGenerator`): a start area built from a fixed seed and saved; walking toward its edge generates the next area ahead of her, spread over several frames, with a reveal effect (a scan wave and a glowing border). Rooms and houses have a purpose and are furnished and lit for it; every door and container can be reached and opened.
- Things to do in them: doors, chests, wardrobes, barrels and crates that open; a minimap and a full map of what has been generated and discovered; monsters that wander the dungeon; villagers who walk the village streets and give way.
- The dungeon as a castle (Mistward Castle):
  - Tiers of their own look: the stone dungeon; Castle of Eternal Mist's blind arches and Medieval Castle's floors for the keep; Dwarven Citadel's halls.
  - Rooms in 12 shapes besides the rectangle, with ceilings that rise in domes, pyramids and steps, from 3 × 3 tiles to 10 × 10.
  - Furniture that never overlaps or blocks a container.
  - Traps, teleporter pads, and prison cells holding prisoners or monsters.
  - Her things where she fell, shown as pictures over a glowing pile.
- Views: third person; a fixed isometric camera that turns in 45° steps and zooms, with roofs and camera-side walls cut away indoors and her silhouette shown through what hides her; a chase camera behind a fighter.

**Combat** (`random_dungeoncombat_level`)
- Three melee weapons with their own attack, flinch and death clips; monsters that notice her, give chase, attack, flinch and die; hit points and damage set by tier so the first area is easier than what is generated in play; regeneration, death and return.
- Blood: bursts of drops and spray from where a blow lands, marks where the drops fall, a pool under a body.
- Wounds: each blow leaves a wound on that one monster, on its skin where the weapon met it.
- Sound placed in the world and fading with distance: her footsteps and voice, each monster kind's steps, growl, roar, cry and death, fires, doors, chests, the rumble of a new section.
- Music: a wandering track over a room tone; a battle track over a tenser atmosphere when a monster comes at her, another one each fight; the wandering track resumes where it left off.

**Magic, the rogue, the choice of hero and a face that moves** (`random_dungeonmagic_level`, `random_dungeonmagic_level2`, `random_dungeonmagic_level3`, `random_dungeonmagic_level4`, `random_dungeonmagic_level5`)
- A fire spell at medium range: a charge of sparks on her hand while she winds up, a bolt of fire that leaves her hand at the release, follows the monster she turned to (one in reach that she can see) and bursts on the first monster or wall it meets: an explosion and a flare of flame, a light that goes with the bolt and flashes where it bursts, the spell's damage on that monster and half of it on the others nearby. The effects are the fire effects `docs/magic.md` lists (DarkHalls, Starter Content), the slow-starting ones in copies that start warm.
- `random_dungeonmagic_level`: a caster (girl-caster-tripo) with three casts made for her; WASD move, R switches between walking and running, left mouse or E cast; the same on an Xbox controller, and the HUD shows the controls of whichever is in use.
- `random_dungeonmagic_level2`: a dark caster (girl-darkcaster-tripo) with a sword in her right hand and fire from her left: three sword cuts and two left-hand casts made for her; left mouse (RB) cuts, right mouse or E (LB) casts while the sword stays in her hand.
- `random_dungeonmagic_level3`: a rogue (Rogue.glb) with a dagger in each hand and a crossbow: three dagger strikes, a crossbow shot and a roll made for her; 1 / 2 or the mouse wheel switch, left mouse (RB) strikes or shoots, B rolls out of a blow. The crossbow bolt is a mesh with a trail that flies straight at the monster she turned to and sticks in the wall where it misses; the weapon not in her hands is carried on her (the crossbow on her back, the daggers on her thighs).
- `random_dungeonmagic_level4`: the hero is chosen before the dungeon loads. A menu level shows each of the four Tripo characters turning on a turntable with her weapons in her hands, beside a card of what she can do: the Warrior wields all weapons, wears all armour, does 30 % more melee damage and has the most hit points; the Mage casts and learns all spells (fire and dark fire) and has the fewest; the Warlock wields all weapons, wears only light and medium armour, casts a limited set of spells (fire) and has balanced hit points; the Rogue wields daggers and ranged weapons, does 30 % more ranged damage, dodges with B and wears only light armour. In the dungeon spells take mana, which comes back slowly; hit points come back only from potions and food; an inventory (I, Tab or Start: the game pauses) holds her weapons, which she wields from there, and what she finds. Each container of the first section holds one of 33 potions and foods (healing, mana, speed, and food that heals over ten seconds), as rare as the item table says. Armour is on the cards only in this level: the armour to find is level 5's.
- `random_dungeonmagic_level5` (the full dungeon game that Mistward Castle was copied from: [the game now](levels/random_dungeonmagic_level5.md#random_dungeonmagic_level5-the-game-now), [spec](spec.md), [design](design.md)): first the Summoner (Summoner.glb, a Tripo model with a rig of generic bone names and no clips) with the Mage's fire spell and the Mage's clips retargeted onto her, and a face that shows what happens to her: six shapes made in Blender (a smile, a frown, the brows down and up, a squint, the jaw) and six patches painted on her texture (eyes shut, happy, screwed up in pain, narrowed in anger; sad brows; an open mouth), mixed into eight expressions. That face has since been replaced by a MetaHuman face for realistic expressions: Epic's preset Aera in her colouring (blond brows, amber eyes), rigged by Epic's cloud service and set on her body under her own anime hair, her painted face cut away by her material; RigLogic moves its 858 blend shapes and its joints from control curves, and sixteen expressions are written from FACS action units (joy, surprise, focus, anger, pain, a shout, disgust, fear, sadness, effort, death, a blink, the eyes looking about). In play she winces when hit, sets her face when she casts, smiles when she finds something, glares while a monster is after her, looks afraid when her hit points run low, shuts her eyes when she dies, and blinks at random. (The face system is still in the C++, but the level as rebuilt from level 4 has no face data: no hero's face moves on it now.)
  - **Now it is level 4's game with a fifth hero and armour to find**: the menu of heroes (the Warrior, the Mage, the Warlock, the Rogue and the **Summoner**, Summoner3.glb, a Tripo model in a bikini with her own walk and run and the Mage's spells cast with her own clips), and 27 sets of armour from Fab warriors, knights, dwarves and orcs (full plate, heavy, medium, light, partial, orc), light, medium or heavy, each refitted in Blender bone by bone to the body of every hero who wears its weight (the Warrior and the Summoner all 27, the Warlock the 17 light and medium ones, the Rogue the 7 light ones, the Mage none) and put on in play from the inventory: the set's mesh follows her skeleton, one set at a time, each blow on her loses the set's share and wears it down; a set of a weight her hero does not wear is refused, and the message says why. A container now holds up to six things (FOUND: the first "and N more"); the inventory scrolls.
  - **And a weapons table and five tiers of rooms**: 29 weapons from the library (short swords, one- and two-handed swords, curved and eastern swords, short blades, bows and crossbows), each with a speed and a damage rating, a durability and a rarity, found in the containers of their tiers: the damage 8 + 3 × the rating, one attack every 1.6 to 0.55 s by the speed, 1 off the durability for every attack that hits, broken and gone at 0. The heroes wield what their cards say (the Warrior and the Warlock all, the Rogue blades and ranged weapons, the Mage and the Summoner none): a found weapon is held where its handle was measured on its model, swung with her own clips or with clips made for it in Blender (a bow shot, a crossbow shot; a two-handed sword is swung as her one-handed sword, with nothing in the other hand: the user's choice). The dungeon's sections are sets of rooms in rings round the start, tiers 1 to 5 (1, 8, 16, 24, 32 sets); a tier past 2 opens only once every set of the tier before is cleared (every room entered, every chest and trunk looted, every monster killed: the dead stay dead), the hallways into a shut tier are barred by gratings, out of tier 5 walled, and a panel under the minimap counts what is left.
  - **And the armour table, the loot table and crafting**: each set has the user's ratings, damage reduction (1-10: a blow takes 5 % less a point) and speed (1-10: 3 % slower a point below 10), a durability (1 off for each blow that reaches her; worn out, it takes off half its share) and the tier of rooms it is found in. Potions and food are found by tier too; 15 crafting materials (ingots, wood, hides and leather, glues, rope, oils) and 5 repair items anywhere. Enter on a repair item lists what it can mend (her armour, the weapons she found) and gives back its share of the full durability. Repair items are made at anvils (one in every forge, at least one in every set of rooms, one in the great hall) from materials, by five recipes; the maps draw the anvils as a small anvil and write each room's tier. The fires' crackle is 8 dB quieter.
  - **And spells, rings, monsters by tier, corpses, allies and an Escape menu**: 30 spells from the user's table (bolts that pierce, slow, burn and jump; wards, armour and a veil; clouds, rifts, a pillar of fire and a meteor storm; curses; a cheat-death pact; the raised dead and a summoned Abyssal Knight fighting at her side), learned from tomes found by tier by the heroes whose card learns their type (the Mage and the Summoner all, the Warlock offense and curses), cast with mana and cooldowns from a bar of ten slots (keys 1 to 0, the wheel). 30 rings (more mana, more hit points, more ranged damage), one of each kind worn. Found weapons broken down into materials at an anvil. One container in three holds anything, then one to six things. The monster gallery's 55 usable monsters in the rooms of their tier, with the tier's hit points, damage, speed and, from tier 3, poison, weakening or slowing; the dead stay where they fell, for good. An Escape menu with four volumes (overall, action, environment, music: kept in the game's settings), another hero, quit.
  - Before that its hero was **Summoner2** (Summoner2.glb, a realistic Tripo model made from two photos, in a red bikini with gold chains): a whole MetaHuman fitted to her model, face and body together (MetaHuman's conform with face landmarks tracked on a portrait of it), its look matched to the photos (pale skin with a pink undertone, dark brown eyes, rosy cheeks, a coral lip tint; long straight hair with a fringe as strands, near-black roots going magenta), the skin of face and body one synthesis so nothing jars between them; her bikini cut from her model and skinned to the MetaHuman's body; the mannequin's motion-captured walk and jog and the Summoner's casts, flinch and death retargeted onto it; the sixteen FACS expressions on its own rig.
- Everything else of the combat level: monsters, tiers, blood, wounds, sound (with spell, crossbow, dagger and roll sounds of its own) and music.

**Mistward Castle** (`mistward_castle`, the dungeon of the current game, reached from Brokenspire's Mistgate: [mistward_castle: the castle](levels/mistward_castle.md#mistward_castle-the-castle))
- Level 5's game as a castle in five themed tiers (the stone dungeon, a castle keep and its cells, dwarven deep halls, then all three), with its name on the menu and the maps.
- Rooms of other shapes, heights and sizes: round, octagonal, hexagonal, triangular, a diamond, L, T and U rooms, domes, pyramids, stepped ceilings and high halls, drawn in their shapes on the maps; small chambers, long galleries and great rooms beside the usual ones.
- Furniture that never overlaps and never blocks a container; containers that stay open (she cannot close one); prison cells that hold a prisoner, a monster or nobody (no straw mound).
- A tier opens at 50 % of the tier before (rooms, monsters, chests); then a boss and its band wait in a room she has seen, and the magic potency ring of the tier before, if she never found it, lies guarded there, both marked on the maps.
- The Warrior's 22 shields (the user's table: damage reduction, speed, durability), found in the containers of their tiers, carried several of a kind, put on and off, broken down at the anvil; armour carried set by set and broken down too.
- A torch made at the anvil, carried in the left hand by every hero, its flame and light with her.
- Thirteen kinds of traps (mechanical, magical, complex, a teleport and a spring plate that throws her across the room) that the Rogue sees and disarms.
- Teleporters between each tier's first set and the great hall, by rings found on their pads.
- Prison cells with prisoners to free (they thank her with a thing and walk off) or monsters that break out.
- All her things dropped where she falls (the shield she started with too), shown as their pictures with a loot effect, taken up again with F.
- The mouse: on the hero menu a click on a name shows that hero and a click on START (or her name again) starts; in the inventory the pointer chooses a row, a click uses it and the wheel scrolls the list.
- The frame rate in the upper left corner in play; a held key or D-pad button walks the inventory; the anvil's lists in alphabetical order; pictures for the found weapons, shields and the torch; every monster kind standing on the floor (Half Creature 05 was drawn under it).
- The third list (also on `random_dungeonmagic_level5`): traps the Rogue has seen outlined in red; the inventory in five tabs (Q / E, LB / RB, a click) with copies of a weapon stacked; ten quick slots on 1 - 0 and the D-pad; the helmet shown or hidden (H); the Warrior's block (right mouse or LT held); her start gear (the dagger, the worst shield, no armour) kept for good; weapons and shields lying on the armories' tables; eight special rooms (a shrine, three fountains, an arena, a trap gauntlet, a vault with its key, a throne room); twenty slashes from bosses that go on her weapons with their own looks and effects.
- The Martial Artist, the menu's fifth hero (and `martial_arts`, a room to try her in): fists and feet, knives, a dodge and a dash, and ten chi attacks, each with its own look on her fists or legs while she makes it (wind, lightning, fire, smoke, stars of light, glowing rings, rods that fly, fireworks, a fireball, chain lightning) and a painted picture of its own; her damage close to the Warrior's with an average weapon.
- A quick bar of any length: every attack she has is on it and the D-pad reaches all of them (past the tenth the bar's ten pictures scroll along); in the inventory she chooses what goes on it and where, attacks and abilities (dodge, dash) alike, and takes things off it.
- The Summoner, the menu's sixth hero (`ai models/Summoner4.glb`): Fire Bolt and Dark Fire with the Mage's damage and effects, half the Mage's mana, daggers that never take a slash, the Mage's equipment (no armour, magic rings, the torch, potions); she summons fighters to her side, two soldiers to begin with, and each tier boss she slays gives her the next summoning (gunners, orcs, alien warriors, spiders, more of each), each cast with a motion of its own.
- Monsters harvested: every kill leaves its parts floating over the body (an essence of its tier or below, its family's part, now and then a hide, leather, glue or oil), taken with F; bosses, usurpers and won arenas give more.
- Potions and scrolls made at the anvil from those parts, offered tier by tier as the tiers open: the 16 healing and mana potions and a scroll for each of the 30 spells, which casts it once at no mana (sharing its cooldown) for a hero who reads that school; Wood Glue in four old recipes; rings and the torch broken down at the anvil too; the fires' crackle at half its old volume.
- Summoned allies that keep apart, a panel of them (how many of how many, each one's hit points) and their marks on both maps; an Escape menu with an Options page (volumes, the controls hint on or off, five resolutions and three display modes); QUIT GAME on the hero menu; the Martial Artist's guard with both elbows out.
- Monsters that shoot: 28 of the 55 kinds also attack from 8 or 15 m in their own theme (darts, rifle shots, rockets, thrown axes, boulders, fireballs, lightning...), for less than their blows and with a longer wait; every kind has a name of its own, and the bars over them show the name only.
- An arena with no way out while it is fought (its doors barred, the ring cold, the pads dark) and an audience: townsfolk appear along its walls before the first wave, cheer when a wave comes and clap and cheer when a monster falls.
- Ceilings of more shapes and sizes: raised, beamed, sail domes, groin, barrel and cloister vaults with ribs, hipped roofs.
- Autoplay: the game plays any hero for a set time (AUTOPLAY on the hero menu, 1 to 30 minutes, or `-Autoplay=<seconds>`), fighting, harvesting, opening chests and exploring until a key of the player's takes over; each hero played her way (the Rogue hides to strike first, the casters and the crossbow step back from what comes close, the Summoner calls her summons first, the best weapon near and far).
- The final boss: once tier 5 is half cleared, Aurorim, an angel of four wings, waits in a room of it with 6 to 10 escorts, scaled as tier 5's boss; he levitates with his wings beating, throws a lance from afar, calls light down from above at middle range and punches with wind round his fists. His death: the best tier-5 thing her hero can use, 2500 gold, the castle conquered, and the door beside him opens back to Brokenspire.
- The Rogue's stealth: Q (LB) hides her for 8 s from a pool of dexterity energy that comes back slowly like mana and fills with any speed potion; monsters lose her, traps stay set under her, and her first blow or bolt out of it does 75 % more.
- The six heroes balanced against each other tier by tier, each growing her own way as the tiers open: the Warrior by what she finds (found weapons carry their tier's power, x 1 to x 2.5), the Mage by her spells, the Warlock by her pact blade and fire, the Rogue by stealth, rings and crossbows, the Martial Artist by her fists and chi (its damage now follows its mana: 23 to 154), the Summoner by her summons; mana comes back as a share of the pool. Measured by trials: each hero at each tier, with what she typically carries there, against a pack of three and the tier's boss under autoplay ([the numbers](levels/mistward_castle.md#mistward_castle-the-castle); [docs/hero-balance.md](hero-balance.md) gathers this preliminary attempt and what a continent-wide one needs).

**Brokenspire** (`brokenspire`, Mistward Castle's starting town, the hero menu opens it: [Brokenspire](levels/brokenspire.md))
- A walled town of 500 m by 500 m generated by rules from a new seed every game (the same town all the game long): the gate in one of its sides (open onto [the wilderness](levels/wilderness.md) since 2026-11-03; barred before), towers along the wall, a cobbled main road to the castle building against the far wall, the square on it with the well, the quest board, four market stalls and the teleport circle beside it, side streets and a lane inside the wall; the trades by the square (smithy, trader, healer, temple, storehouse, tavern), the barracks by the gate, a chapel by the graveyard, the witch's hut in the far corner, a mill and a still, and homes lived in, burned down or boarded up (179 buildings in the saved town before its wall took an irregular outline on 2026-11-02; 100 homes or more since, `884 shape`).
- Every building walked into through a working door (a boarded one's boards pried loose first), furnished for what it is by the dungeon's furnishing, lit, with containers that open; the houses House Forge's timber, stone and thatch, the burned ones in its burnt wood; the castle in the dungeon's own castle walls inside and out.
- The castle building's Mistward Hall, guarded by 5 to 10 monsters of tier 1 that keep to it, and its Mistgate: walked into, it opens Mistward Castle; the castle's arch and its Escape menu bring her back.
- Gold from kills and containers, kept with everything she carries, wears and has learned, her storage and her quests, between the town and the castle; the hero menu starts a new game.
- The services: a trader who buys and sells, a healer, a smith (repairs and his anvil), a temple's blessing, a storage, the teleport circle, the quest board's bounties, each in a panel that pauses the game.
- 32 townspeople on the NPC packs' bodies and clips: the elder, the scholar and the guard captain with questions and 16 quests (two waiting for the wilderness), the trades' keepers, guards, militia on the walls, soldiers, refugees, children, a drunk, the town's fool, a doomsayer, a grieving mother, a witch; each says lines of its own, the wanderers walk the streets and open doors; names and quest marks over them.
- A minimap like the dungeon's and a big map of the town from above (since 2026-11-03: the town's own picture, taken once by a capture straight over it, 4096 pixels over 528 m), with every trade's and place's label and a wax seal on each quest giver, zoomed and moved; until then a painted village map on a torn scroll, and before that an ink map on papyrus with each building as its painted picture.
- The town's life (the third list of 2026-11-02): gardens, pens of farm animals, cooking fires with a pot or a turning spit, the tavern's customers and its server, the smith at his anvil and forge; each townsperson goes between three kinds of work or more, each for a time of its own. Measured (`884 life`): 79 townspeople with a round, 655 jobs done over four minutes of play, no animal ever outside its pen.
- Day and night: a day in an hour of play (a new game begins at 8 in the morning), the sun up from 6 to 18, a moon opposite it, the sky light, the fog's light and the exposure following the daylight; the residents go home at 19 o'clock and everyone in a building sleeps from 22 to 6, a building's torches burning while someone awake is in it.
- The watchtowers and the castle's wall walk climbed by their stairs; falls that hurt (nothing up to 4.5 m, then a share of her hit points growing to all of them at 15 m; in the castle too).
- The list of 2026-11-04 (in every town, the same code; measured in Brokenspire only): farm animals that stop her, the trades' signs painted in Almendra on both faces, the tavern's seats lit, the mill's sails clear of the ground, pens with gates, cobbled streets 2.6 m wide with trodden earth either side, open ground that rises and falls up to 1.4 m (flat under every building), gardens harvested for their crops and bread ovens that bake them into food, food taken from the tables, and a portcullis in the gate raised and lowered by two levers (the other towns' stands raised as she comes to them from outside; only Brokenspire's is kept as she left it).
- The list of 2026-11-07 (in every town, measured in Brokenspire only): the boarded windows' planks solid, training dummies that rock away from a blow or a bolt and stand straight again (measured: the Warrior's sword leaned the dummy 9.1 and the target 8.3 degrees and the Mage's bolt the target 6.2; her bolt at the dummy counted no hit in the last record, its rerun pending), the panels' lists stepped by a held D-pad or key, and the Warrior's block walking her slowly along her facing (measured 88 cm/s forward, 74 back) on every map of the packaged game.

**The wilderness** (round Brokenspire, on its level: [the wilderness](levels/wilderness.md))
- A continent of 300 by 300 km made from one seed as she comes to it: only the chunks of ground within a 2 km square round her exist (72 at the most, 4.5 km²), computed on worker threads and dropped behind her, their memory given back; Brokenspire dropped 2 km off and built again when she comes back.
- 29 terrain templates (the user's 28 and Ashenreach's burned plain; the user's list: grasslands, meadows, farms, savannahs, hills, mountains, peaks, tundras, plains, pine, autumn, aspen and boreal forests, taigas, marshes, wetlands, volcanoes, volcanic tundra, a geothermal caldera with steaming springs, glaciers, arctic land, canyons, mesas, canyon networks, cold steppes, highland moorland, coasts and rocky coasts), chosen by the climate and blended into each other; 29 rivers, 80 lakes, ponds, roads between the towns, farms' fields; each template's plants and rocks, the aspen, birch and acacia made in Blender.
- Vel Morrath, a holy city of Brokenspire's size north-east of it and inland (the cathedral at its middle with its nave, choir, aisles and bell towers, a plaza with a fountain, shrines and statues, a monastery, chapels, the trades, about 90 homes, its archbishop, archivist, templar commander, monks, pilgrims and beggars, its own quests), the stair beneath its altar down into its catacombs (`vel_morrath`: five tiers, each a floor 4.5 m below the last, joined by ramped stairs, her whole state carried down and back); Ashenreach, a forge city in the continent's middle at the edge of a burned plain (the Great Foundry at its middle under siege behind a barricade, open forges, ore carts, workshops, soot-dark houses, the forge-guild's people and quests), its iron gate down into The War Engine (`war_engine`: five tiers of foundry brick and dwarven halls, eleven kinds of machines, [its document](levels/ashenreach.md)); Graymourne, a fog-bound port on the northern coast (an inner harbour of five piers with longships and boats moored, wrecks, the drowned laid on the quay, gibbets, fishers' racks, warehouses, its own thick fog, the harbour's people and quests), the sewer gate in its Sluice House down into the sewers (`sewers`: five tiers going down in damp brick and a vaulted cistern, eleven kinds of drowned sailors and fish-men, [its document](levels/graymourne.md)); villages, camps, lone houses, spawn points, ruins, ritual sites, caves and monster lairs, found and named, the lairs' and spawn points' monsters hunting on open ground.
- A map of the world from above (its own colours, its relief shaded), zoomed from the continent to a kilometre, with a fog of war lifted where she has been, the places' symbols and names, a scroll's torn edge; a minimap of the ground round her; both the town's own in a town's walls.

**Tooling**
- `ue_exec.py` runs a script or a snippet in the running editor; an MCP server exposes the editor's own toolsets.
- `build_cpp.ps1` builds the C++ module without a .NET Framework SDK installed; `package_starship.ps1` packages the fighter as a game, and `package_mistward.ps1` the whole current game (the hero menu, `brokenspire` with its wilderness and cities, `mistward_castle`, `vel_morrath`, `war_engine` and `sewers`) as a Shipping game that needs nothing installed.
- `RDWildProbe` (`-run=RDWildProbe -nullrhi`): the wilderness's ground from a seed, written on a grid without a game, for `wild_seams.py`.
- Shared builders: `interior_lib.py` (rooms, furniture, openables, lights for any building), `mat_lib.py` (textures and Substrate materials from Python), `capture.py` (a picture of the editor world from any camera).
- A survey of the local Fab library: what is usable in UE5 and which animations drive which models (`asset_survey/`, `local-packs.md`); and of the asset directories that hold no Unreal content (2D and pixel-art packs, sound and music, fonts: `asset_dirs_survey.py`, `asset_survey/asset_dirs.json`, listed in `docs/assets.md`).
- A hero gallery, [docs/heroes.md](heroes.md): the library's 100 possible heroes and player characters in ten categories, each with a suggested role and three pictures (full body, upper body and head, an action pose copied from another pack's attack clip onto its own skeleton, with a sword, axe, spear, rifle or bare fists).
- An enemy gallery, [docs/humanoid_enemies.md](humanoid_enemies.md): the library's humanoid enemies (undead and crawlers, orcs and trolls, demons, mutants, beast-men, thugs, soldiers, aliens) in nine categories, pictured the same way; models made of several meshes are posed together, and a pack's own attack clip is used where it has one. Both galleries say for each character which rig it has and which animations it can play (`902` surveys every clip in the project by skeleton and bone names).
- A vehicle gallery, [docs/vehicles.md](vehicles.md): the library's mounts, beast- and hand-drawn carts, siege engines, boats and ships, road vehicles and mine carts, military vehicles and drones, and sci-fi craft, each with its size, a suggested use, its animations and three pictures (three-quarter, side, and a frame of its own clip or a view from above); models from a 60 cm drone to a 115 m cargo ship are staged in one row and shot one at a time with a show-only list.
- A weapons and items gallery, [docs/objects.md](objects.md): the library's fantasy, modern and futuristic weapons, its armour and shields (armour sets: all of a character's armour pieces shown together as an empty suit; shields of every era; helmets and masks) and its non-weapon items (food and drink, medicine, tools and devices, treasure and books), each with its size, a suggested use, three pictures (views chosen from the object's own axes, and a frame of its own clip where it has one: a reload, a shot, a lid) and its animations: its own clips, the first-person arm clips made for it, and the third-person sets in the project that hold that kind of weapon.
- A monster gallery, [docs/monsters.md](monsters.md): the library's 107 monsters ranked by looks from easiest to toughest, each pictured in its own colours and in three recoloured versions (infernal, venom, frost) made by one material that recolours the model's own texture and adds a pattern that follows the body. Packs that exist only in Unity format are unpacked and imported for it.
