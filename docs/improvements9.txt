How it looked:
- No hedges or fences between strips: the open fields were unfenced, and strips were divided only by furrows or boundary stones.
- One crop per field: rotation was decided for each whole field, so every strip in a field grew the same crop that year. A view across a village showed a few very large single-crop areas cut into thin stripes.
- Ridge-and-furrow: ploughing left each strip as a raised ridge, still visible in English pastures today.

design notes for distribution of points of interest and events found along the way:
	Mix types along a route. Variety beats density; the fifth identical bandit camp feels like filler.
	Layer by visibility. Big landmarks are seen from afar and pull players in, mid-size POIs are found along the way, and small vignettes reward people who wander off the road.
	Tie them to the region. A swampy area should produce bog witches, drowned ruins, and leech-infested camps, not generic content, so the space between cities still tells you where you are.
	Let some be dynamic. Roaming encounters on roads make familiar routes feel different on repeat trips.
