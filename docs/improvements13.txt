for these biomes: grasslands, meadows, plains, farms, savannahs, cold steppes, and burned plains, these are a list of scenery that are in them:
Grasslands

Tall grass that hides predators, ambushes and lost objects
Burrow towns of prairie dogs or giant rodents, with collapsing tunnels
A lone tree that serves as a landmark, with a hanged man, a nest or a shrine
Arroyos and dry creek beds that cut hidden paths below the horizon
Glacial erratics: huge boulders stranded in open land, good for climbing and vantage points
Herd migrations that block roads or start stampedes
Wallows and mud holes where tracks pile up

Meadows

Wildflower patches with bees and wild hives, which can mean honey or stings
Brooks with stepping stones, small falls and fishing spots
Fairy rings and mushroom circles
A shepherd’s hut, sheepfold or drystone pen
Reed-fringed ponds with frogs, herons and something under the surface
Wild apple or plum trees left from an old orchard
Butterfly or firefly swarms at dusk

Plains

Rolling swales that hide what’s over the next rise
Buttes and mesas that break the skyline and serve as climbable lookouts
An old battlefield with rusted blades, bones and burial cairns
Milestones, a broken road or a toppled waymarker
A crossroads with a gibbet, shrine or notice post
A wagon wreck with scattered cargo and something that tells the story
Distant storm cells or tornadoes visible for miles

Farms

Hedgerows and stone walls that turn fields into a maze
Scarecrows (are they all just scarecrows?)
Haystacks, a threshing floor and a granary with rats
A mill and millpond, irrigation ditches, a well
Orchards, beehives, a pumpkin patch
Fields at different stages: fallow, sprouting, ripe, burned
An abandoned farmstead with a root cellar and a door that’s locked from the inside
Livestock that wanders, escapes or gets taken

Savannahs

Acacia and baobab trees, which give shade, nests and lookouts
Termite mounds, sometimes taller than a person
Kopjes: rocky outcrops that attract big cats and hold caves
Waterholes where every species comes to drink and fights happen
Kills with circling vultures, which lead the player to danger or loot
Salt pans and cracked dry riverbeds
Smoke from grass fires on the horizon

Cold steppes

Nomad yurt camps with horse herds, trading and hospitality customs
Kurgans and stone figures watching from the hills
Cairns wrapped in prayer cloth where travelers leave an offering
Frozen lakes with cracking ice, trapped things and ice fishing holes
Wind-carved rock and petroglyphs
Hot springs that steam in the cold and make a natural camp
Whiteout blizzards or dust storms that wipe out visibility
Frost heaves and pingos (ice-cored hills)

Burned plains

Charred tree snags, some still standing and some falling
Smoldering hot spots and ember gusts, which act as hazard zones
Ash drifts that preserve footprints, making tracking easy
Early regrowth like fireweed and morels, which signals renewal and gives foraging
Scorched farmsteads and a survivor or looter camp
Fire breaks and ditches that show someone fought the blaze
Fused glass or blackened stone, which hints that this wasn’t a normal fire
Carcasses drawing scavengers, and the creature that started the fire

General tips for any open terrain

Gentle verticality: Small rises and dips control what the player can see, so each crest can reveal something new.
A landmark always in view: A tower, tree or smoke column gives the player something to walk toward.
Moving things: Herds, weather, caravans and patrols make even empty land feel alive.
Small stories: A campfire still warm, a dropped satchel, tracks that split. These are cheap to build and keep players curious.


scenery for these biomes: pine forests, boreal pine forest, taigas, temperate autumn forests, temperate aspen
Pine forests (temperate or montane)

A soft floor of needles that muffles footsteps, good for stealth
Resin-tapping camps with clay pots and scarred trunks, plus a turpentine still
Sunlit clearings where the canopy breaks, with grasses and deer
Rocky outcrops and granite slabs between widely spaced trunks
Lightning-struck pines that are split, charred or still smoking
Logging camps, timber chutes and log flumes running downhill
Woodpecker trees and owl hollows that can hide caches
Pine-cone-strewn ridgelines with long views across the valley

Boreal pine forest (dry, sandy and open)

Pale lichen carpets that crunch underfoot, with reindeer grazing on them
Eskers: long sinuous gravel ridges left by glaciers that make natural raised trails
Sandy dunes held in place by pines
Kettle lakes: round, clear and deep
Old fire scars on trunks and even-aged regrowth stands
Reindeer herder camps, corrals and drying racks
Seid stones and sacred boulders with offerings
The midnight sun or polar night, and auroras over the treetops

Taiga (spruce, fir and larch, wet and dense)

Muskeg and bogs: sinking ground, peat holes and bodies preserved in the peat
Black spruce thickets so dense they’re almost walls
Beaver dams and flooded “ghost forests” of drowned trees
Moose wallows and bear-scratched trunks that warn of territory
Trapline cabins with snares, fur stretchers and a cache on stilts
Larches that turn gold and drop their needles in autumn
Permafrost “drunken forests,” where trees lean every direction as the ground thaws
Frozen rivers used as winter roads, with ice that can crack

Temperate autumn forests (oak, maple, beech)

Deep, noisy leaf litter that hides pits, roots and tracks
Mushroom rings, shelf fungi on dead logs and foraging spots
Old-growth giants with hollow trunks big enough to shelter in
Sunken lanes and holloways worn into the earth by centuries of travel
Mossy stone walls running through the woods from forgotten fields
Acorn and nut caches with squirrels, boars and foragers competing for them
Charcoal-burner hearths, coppiced stools and woodcutter huts
Fog in low hollows at dawn and early dusk
Harvest shrines, carved faces in trees and seasonal festival sites

Temperate aspen groves

White trunks with black eye-like scars that seem to watch you
Clonal groves where a whole hillside is one organism that shares roots, which is great for fantasy
Leaves that tremble and rustle even in still air, so sound is always moving
Carved trunks: names, symbols and dates left by travelers or shepherds
Bright gold stands in autumn set against dark conifers
Elk browse lines and beaver-felled stumps near streams
Sunny, airy undergrowth with wildflowers, berries and grouse
A sharp edge where the aspen meets pine or meadow, a natural spot for ambushes or camps

What helps any forest

Light changes: Shafts of sun, dark stands and sudden clearings set the pace.
Sound: Creaking trunks, birdsong that suddenly stops, distant axes.
Water: Streams, springs and ponds give the player paths to follow and places to stop.
Deadfall and windthrow: Fallen trees create natural barriers, bridges and hiding spots.



list of scenery for these biomes: hills, highland moorland, mountains, mountains and peaks, glacial
Hills

Hillforts: earthen rings and ditches on the summits
Chalk figures cut into the slopes, like giant horses and men visible for miles
Terraced fields and drystone walls following the contours
Holloways and sheep tracks winding between hilltops
Dew ponds: artificial hilltop ponds that never seem to dry up
Folly towers, beacon posts and signal fires on the crests
Quarries cut into a hillside, with abandoned tools and caves behind them
Vineyards, orchards or sheep grazing on sunny slopes
Combes and coves: hidden valleys tucked between hills

Highland moorland

Bogs and peat hags: black channels, sinking ground, will-o’-the-wisps
Tors: wind-sculpted granite stacks on hilltops, good for lookouts and lairs
Heather and gorse that bloom purple and gold and can catch fire
Standing stones, stone rows and hut circles from prehistoric settlers
Peat-cutting banks with stacked turves drying
Rolling mist that wipes out landmarks and gets people lost
A lonely shepherd’s bothy, an abandoned croft or a ruined shieling
Grouse butts, hunting lodges and gamekeeper paths
Streams rushing through peat-stained water, with fords and stepping stones

Mountains

Switchback trails cut into slopes, with mule trains and rockfall
Alpine pastures with summer herders, cheese huts and cowbells
Mountain passes with a fortress, toll gate or hospice monastery
Scree slopes that slide underfoot
Waterfalls dropping off hanging valleys
Mines and adits bored into the rock, with ore carts and tailings
Cliff-side monasteries or shrines reachable only by ladders or ropes
Rope bridges over gorges
Tarns: small dark lakes cupped in rock basins
Eagles, mountain goats and wild sheep on impossible ledges

Mountains and peaks (high alpine, above the treeline)

Knife-edge ridges and arêtes with sheer drops on both sides
Summit cairns, prayer flags and the bones of failed climbers
Thin air that drains stamina and makes the player dizzy
Cornices: snow overhangs that can collapse underfoot
Avalanche chutes and cleared paths from past slides
Couloirs: steep gullies used as climbing routes or death traps
Wind-carved snow, rime-coated rock and ice-glazed ledges
Abandoned climbers’ camps with frozen tents, ropes and journals
Sea-of-clouds views with other peaks rising like islands
Dragon aeries, giant nests or the source of a sacred river

Glacial

Crevasses, some hidden under snow bridges
Seracs: towering ice blocks that can topple at any time
Ice caves with blue light and meltwater tunnels running through them
Moulins: vertical shafts where meltwater drains into the glacier
Moraines: ridges of rock and gravel pushed by the ice
Meltwater lakes, milky-blue rivers and calving ice fronts
Things frozen in the ice: animals, people, ships, ancient creatures
Nunataks: rocky peaks poking through an ice sheet like islands
Snowfields with whiteouts, sun glare and snow blindness
Ice-locked ruins slowly emerging as the glacier retreats

What helps any high terrain

Vertical goals: Peaks pull players upward the way landmarks pull them across plains.
Changing routes: Weather, rockfalls and avalanches can reshape paths.
Rewarding views: Every summit should show the player somewhere new to go.
Safe havens: Huts, caves and hot springs give relief from the cold and exposure.




 list of scenery for these biomes: canyons, canyon networks, mesas:
Canyons

Banded cliff walls whose colored strata read like a timeline, sometimes with fossils exposed
Slot canyons so narrow you can touch both walls, with light shafts from above and flash-flood danger
A river or creek on the canyon floor with cottonwoods, sandbars and fords
Cliff dwellings and granaries tucked into alcoves high on the walls
Petroglyphs and pictographs: handprints, spirals and figures nobody can explain
Natural arches and bridges carved by wind and water
Hanging gardens: ferns and flowers growing where seeps leak out of the rock
Echoes that carry voices, war drums or something calling back
Rockfall debris, toppled boulders and fresh scars where the wall gave way
Rim-to-floor trails, toe-hold steps cut into the rock and old rope ladders

Canyon networks (mazes, badlands and branching gorges)

Dead ends and box canyons, which make good traps and ambush points
Junctions where side canyons split, marked by cairns, carvings or warning signs
Hidden side canyons reachable only by squeezing, climbing or swimming
Pour-offs: dry waterfalls that block upstream travel until you climb them
Potholes and tinajas: rock basins that hold rain water, the only drinks for miles
Outlaw hideouts, smuggler routes and bandit lookouts on the rims
Mineral seams, old mines and gold-panning sites along the washes
Flash-flood debris jammed high in the walls, a warning of how high the water gets
Hoodoos and fins: rock spires and walls creating a stone forest
Distant thunder that means you should leave the canyon floor right now

Mesas

Flat sky islands with sheer sides and their own isolated ecosystem
Mesa-top pueblos or fortresses reachable by one carved stair or hidden path
Buttes and spires: smaller relatives of mesas that make standalone landmarks
Talus aprons: steep rubble slopes ringing the base
Overlooks with views across the desert for dozens of miles
Sky shrines, solstice markers and sun daggers that light up a carving once a year
Wind-scoured caprock with pools after rain and lizards sunning themselves
Eagle and condor nests on ledges below the rim
A lone juniper or piñon clinging to the edge
Storms seen from above, with lightning striking below the mesa top

What helps canyon country

Light as a clock: Walls glow red at sunrise and sunset, and slot canyons go dark early, which signals time to move.
Water as power: Springs, potholes and rivers drive who lives where and what they fight over.
Rims versus floors: Two layers of travel with very different views, threats and routes.
Weather danger: Flash floods turn beautiful terrain deadly, which raises tension without needing enemies.




 list of scenery for these biomes: volcanoes, volcanic tundra, geothermal caldera:
Volcanoes

Lava flows: glowing rivers by night and cooling crusts you can just about walk on
Lava tubes: tunnels left when lava drained away, used as natural dungeons, shelters or highways
Cinder cones: small black hills of loose scoria that slide underfoot
Ash-buried villages with only roofs and church spires poking out
Volcanic bombs: blobs of rock thrown from the crater and scattered across the slopes
Fumaroles: vents hissing sulfur steam with yellow crystal crusts
Obsidian fields of black volcanic glass, good for crafting and dangerous to fall on
The crater rim, with views into a smoking pit or a lava lake
Eruption warnings like tremors, ash fall, dying birds and sudden steam
Fire temples, forges or dwarven foundries built to tap the heat

Volcanic tundra (cold, barren lava land, Iceland-style)

Moss-covered lava fields: soft green blankets over jagged rock that hide holes
Black sand plains and beaches with icebergs washed up on them
Basalt columns: hexagonal pillars forming cliffs, caves and giant “steps”
Glacier volcanoes where the ice melts from below and causes floods
Rift valleys and fissures you can stand inside, where the land is pulling apart
Pseudocraters: small craters formed when lava ran over wet ground
Sod huts, turf-roofed farms and stone sheep pens
Hot rivers in cold air, steaming water running through snow
Auroras over black lava and white snow
Troll rocks: rock formations local legend says are petrified giants

Geothermal caldera

Geysers that erupt on a schedule, which can serve as a natural clock or a puzzle
Hot springs and terraced pools in white, orange and teal mineral steps
Mud pots: bubbling, plopping gray mud that’s boiling hot
Bacterial mats coloring the runoff in bright rainbow bands
Thin crust where the ground looks solid but has boiling water underneath
Steam fields where visibility drops to almost nothing
A crater lake filling the caldera, sometimes acidic, sometimes beautiful
Resurgent domes and ring fractures: the caldera floor rising, a sign of the next eruption
Bathhouses, spa villages and healing shrines built around the springs
Petrified forests or trees killed by heat and gas, standing bare and white
Gas pockets: low hollows where CO₂ collects and kills animals that wander in

What helps volcanic terrain

Visible danger: Steam, glow and smell all warn the player before the hazard hits.
Contrast: Black rock against snow, green moss, turquoise water or orange lava.
A living world: Small tremors, shifting vents and new flows make the land feel active.
Resource rewards: Obsidian, sulfur, rare minerals and fire-touched materials make the risk worth it.




 list of scenery for these biomes: tundras, arctic regions:
Tundras

Polygon ground: frost-cracked earth forming a honeycomb pattern you can see from above
Pingos: ice-cored hills rising out of flat land, used as lookouts or burial mounds
Thaw lakes and ponds by the thousands in summer, which bring mosquito swarms
Caribou or reindeer migrations, with trampled trails, herders following and predators shadowing them
Inuksuit and stone markers that point the way, mark caches or warn of danger
Mammoth bones and tusks emerging from thawing permafrost
Cotton grass, lichen and dwarf willow, a tiny forest at ankle height
Wolf dens, fox burrows, snowy owl perches
Summer bloom: short bursts of wildflowers across the whole plain
Herder camps with tents, sledges, drying fish and antler piles

Arctic regions (ice sheets, frozen seas and polar coasts)

Pack ice and pressure ridges: jagged walls of ice pushed up by shifting floes
Open leads: sudden cracks of black water between ice floes, very dangerous to cross
Icebergs locked in the sea ice, some hollowed into caves
Snow drifts and sastrugi: wind-carved ridges that make walking hard
Seal breathing holes, polar bear tracks and walrus haul-outs on gravel spits
Frozen-in shipwrecks with masts sticking out, logbooks and frozen crew
Ice shelves: cliffs of ice above the sea, calving with a roar
Abandoned expedition huts with supplies, notes and graves
Snow houses and seasonal hunting camps
Whiteouts and polar night, when the horizon disappears
Auroras, sun dogs and halos: strange lights that can guide or mislead

What helps polar terrain

Survival gameplay: Cold, food and shelter give the player constant reasons to keep moving.
Scarce landmarks: Each rock, cairn or ship stands out because nothing else does.
Seasonal change: Summer thaw and winter ice turn the same map into two very different places.
Things preserved in the ice: The cold keeps old stories intact for the player to find.



list of scenery for these biomes: marshes, wetlands, coastal regions, rocky costal
Marshes

Reed beds taller than a person, which hide paths, nests and ambushers
Boardwalks and plank trails, some rotten or deliberately broken
Stilt villages and fishing huts raised above the water
Will-o’-the-wisps and marsh lights that lure travelers off the path
Herons, cranes and flocks that explode into the air when startled
Eel traps, fish weirs and punts tied up in channels
Salt marsh creeks that flood and drain with the tide
Duck blinds and hunters’ hides
Sunken causeways: ancient stone roads now just below the waterline
Drowned churches with only the bell tower showing

Wetlands (swamps, bogs and fens)

Cypress or mangrove swamps with knobby roots, hanging moss and dark water
Peat bogs that preserve bodies, wooden idols and weapons for thousands of years
Floating mats of vegetation that look solid but sink when stepped on
Quicksand and sucking mud
Hummocks: small islands of dry ground with camps, shrines or a witch’s hut
Rotting logs and fallen trees serving as bridges
Pitcher plants and sundews: carnivorous plants, scaled up for fantasy
Swamp gas bubbling up, which is flammable
Crocodiles, snakes, leeches and giant insects
Hidden smuggler or rebel camps that no army can reach

Coastal regions (beaches, dunes and estuaries)

Sandy beaches with driftwood, shells and things washed ashore
Dunes that shift over time to bury or uncover ruins
Tidal flats that open a path at low tide and trap people at high tide
Fishing villages with drying nets, boats pulled up and smokehouses
Lighthouses, watchtowers and signal fires along the coast
Shipwrecks on sandbars with salvage, ghosts and cargo
Lagoons and estuaries with brackish water and mixed wildlife
Salt pans and evaporation ponds
Seabird colonies: noise, eggs and guano
Wreckers’ coves: false lights that lure ships onto rocks

Rocky coastal

Sea cliffs with crashing surf, nesting birds and dizzying drops
Sea stacks and arches: pillars of rock standing offshore
Sea caves reachable only at low tide, used by smugglers or sea creatures
Tide pools full of crabs, anemones and stranger things
Blowholes that spray water high when waves hit
Kelp forests visible just offshore
Seal and sea lion colonies on flat rocks
Clifftop ruins: abbeys, forts or castles slowly falling into the sea
Rock-cut stairs down to hidden coves
Lighthouses on islets reached by causeway or boat
Storm surge: waves that sweep over rocks and drag people in

What helps water terrain

The tide as a clock: Paths open and close, so timing becomes gameplay.
Footing: Mud, rock and water change speed, stealth and combat.
What lies beneath: Drowned ruins and sunken treasure reward the player for looking down.
Sound and fog: Waves, bird cries and mist set the mood and hide threats.
