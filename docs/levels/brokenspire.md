# Brokenspire

The starting town of Mistward Castle (the user's spec of 2026-11-02, [docs/brokenspire.txt](../brokenspire.txt)): a walled town of 500 m by 500 m, generated by rules from a seed of its own for every new game. The hero menu opens it; the castle building's central hall leads into the Mistward Castle dungeon, and the castle leads back. What she carries, her purse, her storage and her quests go with her between the two.

- **Map:** `/Game/RandomDungeon/Maps/brokenspire` (a Save As copy of `mistward_castle`: the same heroes, combat, inventory, HUD and painted interface)
- **Played from:** the hero menu `mistward_castle_select` (START opens the town; AUTOPLAY still opens the castle)
- **C++:** `RD/RDTownPlan.cpp` (the plan and the build), `RD/RDTownGame.cpp` (the town in play, gold, the services, quests, the trip to the castle and back), `RD/RDTownLife.cpp` (the townsfolk's rounds of work, the animals, the spits), `RD/RDTownHUD.cpp` (the minimap, the papyrus map, the panels), `RD/RDTown.h` (`ARDTownsfolk`, `ARDTownAnimal`), `RD/RDGameInstance.h` (what the game keeps between the levels)
- **Scripts:** `881` (the level, its light, the castle's link, the menu), `882` (the pieces, the people, the quests, the build), `883` (the map's pictures), `884` (the test), `885` (the user's list after the first reel), `886` (the third list: the animals, the work clips, the day's hour, falls, the dagger); `blender_numpy/farm_animals.py` and `town_work_clips.py` (the animals and the work clips, made in Blender)
- **Data:** `SourceArt/brokenspire/town.json` (the people and the quests), `SourceArt/brokenspire/map_art.json` (the map's pictures, painted on the InvokeAI server)

## The town

The town is the generator's start section (`bTown`): one section of 168 x 168 tiles of 3 m. The plan (`ARDSection::PlanTown`) draws everything from the section's seed, so the build saved with the map and play's rebuild of its doors and containers agree, and another seed makes another town by the same rules. A new game picks a new seed (`bTownNewSeed`); the game keeps it, so the town is the same every time she comes back from the castle.

The rules, in the town's own frame (from the gate to the castle):

- **The wall:** since the second list of 2026-11-02 it follows an irregular outline (the user's: "the town borders should also be irregularly shaped with the town walls following the shape of the town"): about twenty corners round the town's middle at radii the seed draws (170 to 260 m from it in the saved town), out to the square's edge behind the castle. The Castle Forge pack's stone walls run along each edge, two walls high with a parapet, each edge's pieces stretched to fill it. A square tower stands at every corner, and one more halfway along a long edge. Until then it was a ring round 160 x 160 tiles.
- **The watchtowers** are walked into from the town: an arched door in the face toward the town, a spiral stair of twelve quarter turns inside, and at the top (since the third list of 2026-11-02: "the circular stairway just ends, there is no platform or floor") a stone floor over the whole tower but the stairwell, with a metre-high balustrade round the stairwell's open part, under the tower's roof on four posts over the parapet. The stairwell stays open over the stair's last three quarter turns, where her head needs the room; the quarter she steps off onto (the landing) and the corners are floored.
- **The gate:** on a straight stretch of the outline across the main road, a tower each side, in one of the four ways (the seed's choice). It is shut and barred (F: "the wilderness beyond is not safe yet"); the road runs on outside it.
- **Mistward Castle:** against the far wall, at the end of the cobbled main road, behind a forecourt. Four rooms: an entry hall, the **Mistward Hall** behind it (11 by 12 tiles, its back wall the **Mistgate**) and two wings (an armory and a library). Inside, its walls are the dungeon's own Castle theme. Outside (since 2026-11-02; until then the dungeon's blind arches, inside and out) it is a medieval castle of Castle Forge's stone:
  - two storeys of wall up to a **wall walk**: arrow slits in about one segment of three below; above, small windows, now and then a tall one, a slit or plain stone (by a hash of the place, not the plan's stream);
  - crenellated **battlements** round the walk, which is the whole roof and is walked on;
  - a **round tower** at each corner, 6.3 m across and 12.6 m high under a cone roof, with slits and windows facing out;
  - **the way up** (since the third list of 2026-11-02: "one watch tower on the castle let the player pass through to the inside ... the player should only be able to get to the watch tower from the inside of the castle ... there is also no visible door from the top of the watch tower to the battlements ... there should be a way down from the battlements to the floor of the castle, like stairs"): a stone stair inside the castle, in the wing on the front tower's side, against its outer wall (30 steps of 22 cm over three tiles, 1.6 m wide), rises through a stairwell in the roof (the two tiles over its upper steps have no ceiling and no roof; a metre-high stone balustrade round the opening on the walk). Every tower is entered from the walk by a doorway toward the castle, has a floor at the walk's height and a torch inside. No tower has a door at its foot; until then the front one had a door from the forecourt and a spiral stair up to the walk, the only way onto it.
- **The town wall** is plain stone with an arrow slit in one upper segment of three. Its square towers have slits and small windows. Until 2026-11-02 every segment was Castle Forge's `Wall_02a`, which has an arched doorway through it: a row of arches.
- **The square:** on the main road, about a third of the way in, crossed by a cobbled road from wall to wall. In it: the well, the quest board, four market stalls, four fire baskets. Beside it, the **teleport circle** (a ring of standing stones round the castle's teleporter glow).
- **Streets** (since the second list of 2026-11-02: "cobblestone streets should be straight and the dirt roads should be meandering, curved and wandering"):
  - the main road from the gate to the castle, the cross road through the square, the square, the forecourt and the stair turret's path: straight and cobbled;
  - the earth roads (since the third list of 2026-11-02: "they still form a regular grid. they should meander around the town in a semi-random manner"): about fifty places scattered over the town (12 tiles or more apart, clear of the square, the castle and the lane), joined to the cobbled roads and to each other by the shortest tree and about fifteen loops besides, each join a way that sets off up to 35 degrees from the straight line and drifts with two slow waves of its own, straightening as it nears its end; the tree's ways a road wide, the loops narrower. Measured (`884 shape`): 29 % of their length runs within 12 degrees of the town's axes (a grid: nearly all), 2 km of them. Until then six to eight streets along the frame's axes, each wavy;
  - a trodden lane two tiles wide all along the inside of the wall.
- **The ground** is drawn a vertex a metre. The earth is the ground material's tan layer, so it stands out from the grass, worn darker in patches. Every road's edge is ragged by noise, and the roads' surfaces rise and sink a few cm (earth up to 5, cobbles 2.5), shaded by normals from the heights. The bumps are seen only: everyone walks on the flat floor.
- **The trades:** the temple, the tavern, the smithy, the trader's store, the healer's house and the storehouse take the plots nearest the square; then the barracks nearest the gate, the chapel nearest the graveyard (in a corner of the town away from the gate) and the witch's hut farthest from the square.
- **Homes:** every plot left along the streets, each door toward its street. Each is lived in (about 55 %), burned or boarded up (about 25 %: planks over its windows and its door until she pries them loose with F).
- **Burned houses** are about 20 %. Each has charred beams and rubble inside and no roof at all. Each wall segment stands one storey, two or none, and an upper storey only on the one below (since the second list of 2026-11-02, after the user saw roof pieces and walls standing on nothing).
- **Windows:** a hidden box covers every window piece's whole bounds; the pieces' own collision missed their sills, and she could walk through the ledges.
- **Signs:** each trade's hanging sign says what it is on both faces of its board: GENERAL GOODS, SMITHY, HEALER, TEMPLE, STOREHOUSE, TAVERN, CHAPEL, BARRACKS, HEDGE WITCH.
- **Outdoors:** the mill (Old Windmill's) and the still (a copper pot and kegs beside the tavern), the graveyard (gravestones in rows inside an iron fence with a gate), the refugees' tents by the gate, the barracks' training dummies, carts, hay, sacks and logs by the homes, trees, bushes and grass.
- **The short grass and small bushes** (the third list: "there should be short grass in more places where there aren't any roads, there should be more small bushes"): two to four clumps of Hive Sherwood's short wild grass on every free tile of ground (one or two beside a road), a small bush on about one tile in six, flowers on a few; about 23 000 clumps and 1 150 bushes, faded out from 45 m (the pieces' `CullDistance`) without shadows.
- **Gardens, pens and cooking fires** by the lived-in homes (the third list: see [the town's life](#the-towns-life)).

Every building is walked into through a working door. Its inside is furnished by the dungeon's own furnishing for a room of its kind (the smithy a forge with its anvil, the temple and the chapel pews and an altar, the barracks and the healer's house beds and chests, the store and the storehouse shelves, barrels and crates, the tavern a kitchen with tables and stools, the homes a kitchen or a room of chests), lit by torches, with containers that open with F. The houses are House Forge's timber, stone and thatch modules, two storeys of walls high under a gabled roof.

Built for the seed `-1138391333` (the saved one) before the irregular wall: 179 buildings (182 rooms), 186 doors, 789 containers, about 30 000 instanced meshes, 611 lights, in about 0.7 s. With the irregular wall (the second list of 2026-11-02) the saved build is 23 206 instanced meshes and 475 lights; the town inside the outline is smaller (about 87 buildings with lights in the tests).

## The town's life

The user's third list of 2026-11-02: "the town should look more lively ... small gardens with plants ... small animals like chickens, rabbits, goats, cows and pigs in pens, cooking fires with pots on them being tended by a villager, cooking fires with meat on spits being turned by a villager, taverns with customers in them being served by villagers, forges being worked by a blacksmith ... have a finite state machine that loops between at least 3 activities for NPCs and have different timers for each NPC so that they drift between at least 3 different jobs".

- **By the homes** (`ARDSection::TownLife`, the plan's outdoor step 4; up to 18 gardens, 12 pens, 8 pots and 6 spits): beside a lived-in home, a tile from its walls and off the roads and door fronts,
  - a **garden** (2 x 1 or 2 x 2 tiles): a low plantation fence with a way in toward the house, dark soil, rows of three or four kinds of plant (wild carrot, wheat, pumpkins, flowers, a ground plant; sunflowers along the back of a deep bed);
  - a **pen** (2 x 2 tiles, 3 x 2 for the cows): a wood fence all round, a trough and hay, trodden earth; its animals the kinds in turn (hens, pigs, goats, cows, rabbits, then their other colourings);
  - a **cooking fire** (two tiles): Hive Sherwood's campfire with its flames and light and firewood beside it; a cauldron on the furniture kit's tripod, or a spit on two posts whose rod and meat turn in play while someone turns its crank.
  A home with any of these always has someone living in it. The refugees' camp has a pot over its first tent's fire, a spit over the second's and a fire to talk by at the third.
- **The animals** (`ARDTownAnimal`; `blender_numpy/farm_animals.py`: hen, rabbit, goat, pig and cow, made in Blender: blended shapes, per-part colours, two or three colourings each, a skeleton and three clips each, Idle, Graze and an in-place Walk with its stride's ground speed measured on the planted feet): 2 to 8 to a pen by kind. Each stands, grazes (pecks, nibbles) and walks to another place in its pen in turn, each for a while of its own, kept inside by its body's half length. There were no farm animals in the project's packs, the asset library or the Fab vault.
- **The tavern:** up to six small tables, a stool each side; each stool a seat (SitDrink or SitTalk), each table a place to serve from, the bar's front a place to take a tray and to stand and talk. Customers (grown townsfolk's bodies, about 70 % of the seats) go between two seats and the bar; the innkeeper and a server (with three tables or more) between the bar and the tables, the tray in their hands on the way (CarryWalk) and at the table (CarryIdle).
- **The smithy:** the smith (Dwarf_Pack's Broddi) hammers at his anvil (his pack's forge clip, a hammer in his hand), stands at the forge's fire and by his door for her.
- **The well and the market:** three places to chat about the well, a buyer's place before each stall.
- **The rounds of work** (`ARDTownsfolk`'s state machine: none, going (a tile path to the walkable tile nearest the place), gliding (the last steps straight onto it, through a fence's way in or between stools), working (its clip, its prop: a ladle, a tray, a mug, a bowl, a hammer), strolling about its post): each townsperson with a round goes between its places in turn, each for a time drawn from its own range (a least time from 14 to 24 s, a most 1.6 to 2.6 times that: no two the same), and passes over a place someone else has. The residents' rounds are their home's garden, pen or fire and the nearest of each kind they lack, a chat at the well or the market and a stroll; the children's a pen, a chat and play; the refugees' their camp's pot, spit and fire; the drunk a seat, the bar and a stroll; the preacher and the fool the well, the market and a stroll. Every round has three kinds of work or more. The residents work from half past six to seven in the evening; those in a building sleep from 22 to 6 where they are (a seated customer keeps their seat).
- **Measured** (`884 life`, test_t3g, seed `-1138391333`): 18 gardens (1 251 plants), 12 pens (46 animals of all five kinds), 9 pots and 7 spits with the camp's, 6 seats, 7 places to serve; 79 townspeople with a round (with the tavern's customers and server), every round three kinds of work or more, no two with the same range of seconds; over four minutes of play at 11 o'clock (the game three times as fast) 655 jobs done, every group of place worked (gardens tended in 824 of the two-second samples, pens fed 480, pots stirred 419, seats sat on 768, tables served 93, the smith at his anvil and forge, spits turned 214: their meat turning), 30 of the 79 with three kinds done; no animal ever outside its pen. A place of work on a blocked tile (the bar's front) is also a place a walk can start from: the tile path starts at the walkable tile nearest her (until then the server at the bar never reached a table).
- **The work clips** (`blender_numpy/town_work_clips.py`, on the NPC packs' UE5 mannequin, imported by `886` onto the NPC_Characters_Pack's skeleton, every pack's mannequin made compatible with it): Stir, Crank, Tend, Scatter, SitDrink, SitTalk, CarryWalk, CarryIdle, Talk. The packs have no work clips of their own (none in the project, none in the library's survey); the smith's are Dwarf_Pack's forge clips, the drunk's Drunk_Man_Animations' chair sit and talk.

## Day and night

A full day passes in an hour of play in the town (the third list of 2026-11-02: "the day/night cycle should last one hour, not half an hour"; half an hour until then; `bDayNight`, `DayLengthSeconds` 3600, `RD/RDTownDay.cpp`). A new game begins at 8 in the morning.

- **The sun** (the level's atmosphere sun) rises at 6, stands 62 degrees high at noon and sets at 18. It is warmer near the horizon, and it casts no shadow once it is down.
- **A moon** made in play crosses the sky opposite it. It is the atmosphere's second light: cold, dim, without shadows.
- **The rest of the light follows the daylight:** the sky light (8 % of the day's at night), the height fog's light and the exposure (1.2 stops less at night). The lamps and torches burn all day.
- **The clock** stands still under a panel's pause and while she is in the castle, which is indoors. The game keeps the hour between the two (`URDGameInstance::TownHour`, `TownDay`).
- **On screen:** the minimap shows "day 1 · 14:30" under it, and the map's key shows it too.
- **For a reel or a test:** `-RDTownHour=<h>` starts a new game at another hour; `SetTownHour` sets the hour in play.
- **The townspeople's day** (the second list): the residents go into their homes at 19 o'clock and come out at half past six. Everyone inside a building sleeps from 22 to 6.
- **The lit buildings:** a building's torches and their flames burn while someone awake is inside it ("unless the NPC is sleeping"). Every building someone can be in has a torch inside its door. The castle is always lit. Measured (`884 lights`, test_n3): at noon 15 buildings lit (the keepers' trades and the homes whose residents had wandered in), at 20 o'clock 26; at 23 o'clock the sleepers' buildings dark (20 of them in an earlier run).

## The castle's hall and the way into the dungeon

The Mistward Hall is guarded by 5 to 10 monsters of tier 1 (their number and kinds from the town's seed). Each keeps to the hall (`ARDMonster::HomeRoom`): it wanders only there, and takes no foe outside it, so she can fight them in the hall or walk out. Killed, they stay dead for the game.

Walking into the Mistgate (within 1.7 m of the hall's back wall) opens Mistward Castle. In the castle, the arch in its great hall (F: "return to Brokenspire") and the Escape menu's **Return to Brokenspire** bring her back, before the castle's door.

## Gold and her things between the levels

The game keeps (in `URDGameInstance`, while the program runs):

- **her things:** every found weapon with what is left of it, everything carried, the armour, shield and rings she wears, the spells she learned from tomes, the chi attacks she gained, her hit points and mana, a blessing on her;
- **her purse:** gold from kills (1 to 4 times the monster's tier, a boss 40 times its tier more) and from containers (one in about two, 3 to 12 times the tier), in the castle and in the town;
- **her storage** at the storehouse, the town's seed, the containers she opened there, the castle's tiers she opened (they stay open) and the containers of its first set of rooms she opened, the Hall's guards she killed;
- **her things where she fell:** a pile of hers left lying when she goes is laid again where she comes back into that level (in the castle beside the arch in the great hall, in the town in the square);
- **the tiers' bosses she killed:** a tier open from an earlier visit gets its boss again (once a room of the tier before is seen) unless she killed it, and its guarded ring unless she has it;
- **the quests and their counters.**

The hero menu starts a new game: nothing carried, an empty purse and storage, no quests, a new town.

## The services

Each building's service is at its counter, basin, altar, chest or anvil, and its keeper (F at them) opens it too. A panel opens and pauses the game: W / S, the wheel or the D-pad choose, Enter, E, a click or A does it, A / D or LB / RB change a list's tab, Escape, F, Tab, Backspace or B close it.

## The people

The elder, the scholar and the captain have functionality: questions (why the town needs her, what the evil is, the wilderness) and quests. The keepers open their buildings' services. Everyone else says a line of their own and turns to her. Those who wander (the children, the town's fool, the militia on the lane, the refugees, the preacher, the residents) walk the town's tiles and open the doors on their way.

Their walk (the second list of 2026-11-02: "the children NPCs' legs do not move when they walk"):
- **The clip:** each pack's forward walk (`MM_Walk_Fwd`) with its root held in place (`AS_<pack>_WalkLocked`). The packs' `MM_Walk_InPlace` moves no foot forward or back at all (measured: 0.0 cm), so they had marched on the spot while they slid.
- **The speed:** each walker moves as far as its feet do, at its body's size (a child at 0.8 strides 0.8 as far). Measured following them: the planted foot moves 29 to 45 cm/s while the body moves 148 to 185.

The NPC Characters pack's `_Full_02` bodies come without a head (Simple Tam, Gorric, Father Odran, Old Pell had none). They wear the pack's separate head, hair, beard and moustache as parts. A name hangs over each person near her, with a gold **!** over one who has a quest for her and **?** over one who is waiting for one she has done.

## Quests

A quest's goal is a counter the game keeps across the levels; its progress is what the counter has gained since she took it (the highest castle tier open, and the Hall's guards killed, count whole). A quest done is turned in to whoever gave it. The tracker on the right of the screen shows her purse and her quests, in the town and in the castle.
